Apple started accepting iPhone Air trade-ins this week alongside the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone Duo launch, and the numbers are telling. [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/10/iphone-airs-200-premium-disappears-when-you-trade-it-in/) caught what a lot of people are about to discover the hard way: the $200 premium you paid for the Air over the base iPhone 17 evaporates entirely when you go to trade it in.

This isn't a bug. Apple's trade-in program has always valued phones by capability, not by what you paid. The Air uses the same A18 chip as the iPhone 17, the same camera system, the same storage tiers. The only material difference is the thinner chassis and slightly larger display. Those don't move the resale needle because they don't change what the phone can do. A buyer getting a refurbished unit in 2027 isn't paying extra for 6.6mm vs 7.8mm of thickness.

The timing stings because Apple just raised iPhone prices across the board. The Air launched at $999 in 2025, positioning itself as the new aspirational midpoint between the base model and the Pro. That was fine when the base iPhone 17 stayed at $799. But this week's iPhone 18 lineup bumped everything up $100, so now you're paying $1,099 for an Air and getting iPhone 17 trade-in value back. It's a rough deal if you're on the annual upgrade cycle, which is exactly the customer Apple cultivated with the iPhone Upgrade Program.

The Air was supposed to be the phone for people who wanted Pro aesthetics without Pro features or Pro pricing. It succeeded at that, and it sold well. But Apple built its trade-in structure around functional distinctions, and the Air doesn't have any that matter in a resale context. It's a design object that depreciates like a spec sheet.

If you bought an Air last year planning to trade up this year, you just funded Apple's margin twice.

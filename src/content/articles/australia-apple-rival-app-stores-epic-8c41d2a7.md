Australia has joined the list of places where Apple no longer gets to decide, on its own, how apps reach an iPhone. On Thursday, Australian time, a Federal Court judge ordered Apple to let Australian users install apps from competing app stores and straight from websites, and to let developers take payments outside Apple's system. The big changes start **March 1, 2027**. One small change starts today: Fortnite is coming back.

The orders, reported by [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/22/rival-app-stores-in-australia-ordered/), [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/21/apple-must-open-iphone-to-alternative-app-stores-in-australia-after-epic-games-ruling/) and [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1016397/australia-orders-apple-to-open-the-iphone-to-third-party-app-stores), come from **Justice Jonathan Beach** in Epic Games' long-running antitrust case. They follow the court's August 2025 finding that Apple misused its market power by restricting how apps are distributed and paid for. 9to5Mac says the new decision runs 235 pages.

## What Apple has to allow

The orders cover iPhone users whose Apple Account region is Australia. Developers can be based anywhere, as long as they're in Apple's Developer Program.

- **Rival app stores and web downloads.** Users must be able to find, download, install and update apps through competing marketplaces and websites, and Apple can't charge for that access or get in the way of it.
- **The same tools for everyone.** Developers distributing outside the App Store have to get the technical tools they need on substantially the same terms as App Store developers, and Apple can't penalize them for using other stores or payment services.
- **Outside payments, even inside App Store apps.** Developers can use other payment systems and link out to them. Apple can't require them to also offer its own in-app purchase system, report those transactions, or let Apple audit them, and it can't stop them from charging less outside its system. 9to5Mac notes those reporting and side-by-side requirements are ones Apple imposes in Brazil and Japan.
- **Limits on fees.** Apple can't charge anything on outside purchases in apps distributed outside the App Store. For App Store apps, its fee on outside purchases is capped at what it charges for physical goods and services, plus genuine, reasonable extra costs it incurs for digital purchases.
- **Preinstalled stores are allowed.** Apple can't ban third-party apps or rival app stores from being preinstalled on iPhones, though it doesn't have to carry competing stores inside its own App Store.

## What Apple gets to keep

Apple's wins are narrower but real. The judge refused Epic's request to make the orders worldwide, so they stop at Australia's borders. And security checks stay: apps installed from outside the App Store still need an automated security or privacy scan and notarization by Apple or a qualified third party.

There are guardrails on that, too. Apple can't use security as a reason to throw up disproportionate installation hurdles, any extra security rules for outside payments have to be necessary and proportionate and explained to developers in advance, and Apple can only remove or disable an externally installed app in specific cases involving harmful content, genuine security risks or legal requirements.

## Fortnite skips the line

Most of this waits until 2027, but the order to readmit Fortnite and other eligible Epic apps to the Australian App Store doesn't. According to 9to5Mac, it takes effect right away, with Apple required to act once Epic applies in writing. Epic told Reuters, via MacRumors, that Fortnite returns to the Australian App Store today. Australia had been the one country left out when Fortnite came back to the App Store worldwide in May, because Epic held off while it pushed for court orders on Apple's payment terms.

## Apple's answer

Apple told Reuters it will appeal and ask the court to pause enforcement, pointing to its intellectual property rights and App Store security.

## What this means

- **Nothing changes for most Australian iPhone owners until March.** The new rules kick in at noon Melbourne time on March 1, 2027. Apple can ask between February 1 and February 15 for a court-approved extension to May 3.
- **An appeal could stretch the timeline.** If the court grants Apple's request to pause enforcement, those dates could slide.
- **The fee cap is the part to watch.** Rival stores only matter if developers can make money in them. Tying Apple's cut on outside purchases to what it charges for physical goods is the court's attempt to keep the "open" door from coming with a steep toll.
- **It's another country on a growing list.** The Verge points out Australia follows similar changes in the EU, Japan and Brazil. Each one has its own rules, which means developers are building for a patchwork, and Apple is fighting the same argument in a new courtroom every few months.

[AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/10/30/i-rely-on-this-apple-maps-feature-tons-of-people-dont-know-exists?utm_source=rss) wrote about Share ETA in Apple Maps, calling it the feature that converted them from Google Maps. They're not wrong to highlight it.

The ability to tap a button and send someone a live-updating link to your location and arrival time is the kind of thing that sounds minor until you actually use it. No group text with "5 min out" updates that are already stale by the time you send them. No switching to a separate tracking app. Just a persistent notification on the recipient's phone that updates as traffic changes or you take a detour. It works for the person driving to pick you up at the airport, for your partner wondering when you'll be home with dinner, for coordinating pickups with kids' activities.

Google Maps has similar functionality, but it's buried in location sharing settings and feels like overkill when you just want to share one trip. Apple's implementation is trip-specific and ephemeral - the sharing stops when you arrive. That's the right default. It also sends the ETA as an actual notification that recipients can tap to open Maps and follow along, rather than requiring everyone to already have the app open or check a specific screen.

The feature debuted in iOS 13 back in 2019, which means it's been around for five years and a lot of people still have no idea it exists. That's on Apple's communication, or lack thereof. There's no onboarding callout, no tips notification. You have to either stumble across the share button during a drive or hear about it from someone else. For a feature that could genuinely pull people away from Google Maps - which still has better POI data and search in most cities - that's a missed opportunity.

If you haven't used Share ETA yet, try it next time you're running late. It won't fix Apple Maps' occasional routing weirdness, but it might make you less likely to reach for Google.

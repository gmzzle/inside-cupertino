Three days before the iPhone Duo hits store shelves, Apple wants you to know where some of it comes from. On **Tuesday, October 20**, the company [announced](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/apple-announces-american-manufacturing-program-partners-for-latest-iphone-lineup/) two new deals under its American Manufacturing Program (AMP): a titanium supplier in Pennsylvania for the foldable Duo, and a bigger role for a Delaware company building part of the iPhone 18 Pro's new camera.

It's a real supply-chain story, not just a press-release victory lap. But if you're one of the people staring at a December or January delivery date, read the fine print first.

## The two deals

**Perryman Company, titanium for iPhone Duo.** Apple says it will commit **up to $100 million over the next three years** to Perryman, a family-owned specialty titanium maker. At its plant in Houston, Pennsylvania (not the Texas one), Perryman will melt, forge and roll the **grade 5 titanium** used in the Duo's enclosure, with a minimum of **80% recycled content**, according to Apple. [Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/apple-commits-up-to-100-million-for-us-titanium-in-domestic-manufacturing-push-4959914) describes it as a commitment to purchase up to that amount of titanium. Apple says the metal will go into other products too, not only the Duo.

**Qnity Electronics, camera films for iPhone 18 Pro.** Qnity, which joined AMP in March, will now produce the "advanced films" that make the iPhone 18 Pro's **variable aperture** work. That's the feature in the new 48MP Fusion Main camera that lets the lens physically open wider in low light and close down in bright scenes, for more control over light and depth.

Apple COO **Sabih Khan** said in the release: "American ingenuity is vital to all our products, from the titanium crafted for iPhone Duo to breakthrough camera innovations in iPhone 18 Pro." CEO **John Ternus** also highlighted the announcement on X, [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/20/apple-touts-iphone-duo-18-pro-parts-made-in-usa/) noted.

## The bigger number: nearly 100 U.S.-made parts

The headline deals sit inside a broader claim. Apple says **nearly 100 components** in the iPhone Duo and iPhone 18 Pro are made by American companies, drawn from suppliers in more than a dozen states. It used the release to update several existing partners:

- **Corning** in Harrodsburg, Kentucky: by the end of 2026, Apple says **all** cover glass for iPhone, including the Duo, and Apple Watch sold worldwide will come from this one facility.
- **TSMC Arizona**: on track to make **more than 100 million chips** for Apple in 2026, with construction continuing on its north campus.
- **Broadcom** in Fort Collins, Colorado: a $1.5 billion upgrade, announced in July, for radio-frequency parts, under a deal Apple says will produce more than 15 billion U.S.-made chips.
- **Coherent** in Sherman, Texas: the lasers behind Face ID, including on the iPhone 18 Pro's redesigned Dynamic Island.
- **Amkor** in Peoria, Arizona: a chip packaging and test plant still under construction, with Apple as one of its first customers.

All of this rolls up into the **$600 billion, four-year U.S. commitment** Apple made when it launched AMP last August. As [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/20/apple-adds-us-titanium-supplier-for-iphone-duo.html) points out, final assembly of most iPhones still happens overseas. These deals are about components and materials, not putting together phones in the U.S.

## What it doesn't fix

The timing is hard to miss. CNBC reports that on the first day of preorders, Morgan Stanley saw delivery estimates for some Duo models stretch to roughly **14 weeks**, the longest wait the firm has recorded for a new iPhone in a decade. JPMorgan analysts expect Apple to ship about **8 million to 9 million** Duos this year, down from earlier expectations of about 10 million, because of supply limits. Apple, meanwhile, has started capping U.S. buyers at two Duos each.

Apple did not say whether the Perryman deal adds near-term capacity, per CNBC. A three-year titanium commitment is a long-term supply move. Nothing in today's announcement suggests it changes how many Duos ship before the holidays.

## What this means

- **Your delivery date isn't moving because of this.** If you have a Duo order in, check the Apple Store app rather than expecting a U.S. titanium bump. Some early orders have been moved up in recent days, but that's separate from today's news.
- **The Duo's body is a recycled-titanium story.** Apple is putting a number on it: at least 80% recycled content in the titanium Perryman supplies, which fits its goal of a carbon-neutral supply chain by 2030.
- **The variable aperture camera has a U.S. piece.** If the iPhone 18 Pro's camera is why you're upgrading, a key part of it now has domestic production behind it.
- **Expect more of these.** Apple has now tied U.S. suppliers directly to its two flagship phones. With Mac mini production slated to start at its Houston, Texas, facility this year, the next round of AMP announcements could easily be about Macs.

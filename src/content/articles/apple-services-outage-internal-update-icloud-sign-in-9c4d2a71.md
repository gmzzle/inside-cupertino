If your iPhone spent Tuesday morning asking for your Apple Account password, it wasn't you. A long list of Apple services went down starting around 8:15 a.m. Pacific, and Apple says the cause was its own software, not an attacker.

"Many of our systems are back up and running after an internal update caused some outages this morning," an Apple spokesperson said in a statement given to Bloomberg, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/apple-services-including-icloud-app-store-and-apple-music-hit-by-outage/), and [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/corporations/apple-outages-working-get-systems-back-online-rcna609861). "We're working to get the remaining systems online as soon as possible."

## What broke

Apple's [System Status page](https://www.apple.com/support/systemstatus/) tells most of the story. As of about 3 p.m. Pacific on Tuesday, it listed:

**Still ongoing**

- **iCloud Account & Sign In**: "may be slow or unavailable," affecting all users, since 8:45 a.m.
- **iCloud Web Apps (iCloud.com)**: unavailable for some users since 8:45 a.m.
- **Apple Business** and **Apple School Manager**: slow or unavailable for some users since 10:40 a.m.

**Resolved**

- **App Store**: 8:45 a.m. to noon
- **AppleCare on Device**: AppleCare purchases unavailable for all users, 8:15 a.m. to 12:39 p.m.
- **Volume Purchase Program**: purchase problems from 8:45 a.m. to noon
- **iCloud Mail** and **Schoolwork**: marked resolved, with no end time posted

TechCrunch also reported earlier problems with Apple Music. On Reddit, some people said they couldn't activate a new iPhone, which lines up with the sign-in failure. Activation needs your Apple Account. NBC News reported that staff at Apple Stores in New York and Chicago said their internal systems were down for over an hour and they couldn't complete purchases or book appointments.

## Why it felt worse than a normal outage

Most service outages are invisible until you open the broken app. A sign-in outage is different. When iCloud can't confirm who you are, your devices start asking for your password, and anything that checks your account, from the App Store to iCloud Mail, can fail at the same time.

The timing didn't help. The outage came days after the Microsoft cloud failure that took down Outlook and Teams, a brief outage of Chase's banking app, and a partial outage of the NSA's internal classified network, TechCrunch noted. None of those have been blamed on cyberattacks, and Apple was quick to say the same about its own.

## What to do if you're still seeing problems

1. **Don't reset anything.** Repeated password prompts during an outage don't mean your account was compromised. Resetting your password or signing out of iCloud now can leave you stuck until the service recovers.
2. **Dismiss the prompt and wait.** If your password is rejected, check [Apple's status page](https://www.apple.com/support/systemstatus/) before trying again.
3. **Hold off on setting up a new device.** If you're activating an iPhone or Mac, leave it until iCloud Account & Sign In shows green.
4. **Retry failed purchases later.** App Store and AppleCare purchases are back, so anything that failed this morning should go through now.

Apple hasn't said what the internal update was or why it hit so many services at once. Some Apple services were still listed as down at the time of writing. We'll update this story if Apple shares more.

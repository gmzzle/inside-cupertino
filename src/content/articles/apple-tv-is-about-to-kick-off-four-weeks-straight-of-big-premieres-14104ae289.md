[9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/10/apple-tv-is-about-to-kick-off-four-weeks-of-big-premieres/) notes Apple TV+ is dropping big premieres four weeks in a row starting next week. That might sound like basic programming, but for Apple it represents something closer to actual strategy.

The run kicks off with Slow Horses season 5, which is legitimately good television and one of the few Apple shows that critics and regular humans both like. Following that are three more premieres Apple's betting will keep people subscribed through October. It's the opposite of their early approach, which was basically "dump everything in spring, go silent for six months, wonder why churn is high."

Apple's spent years figuring out what Netflix learned in 2013: you need a calendar. Not just shows, but a reason for people to keep paying $9.99 every single month. They've clearly studied how HBO kept subscribers hooked by staggering Game of Thrones, Succession, and The Last of Us so there was always something worth staying for. Slow Horses is Apple's closest thing to that kind of anchor.

The fall slate also includes Silo, Shrinking, and whatever else Tim Cook's content team thinks will move the needle. Some of it will land, some won't, but the cadence matters more than individual wins. Apple's figured out that having one excellent show people actually talk about is worth more than six forgettable ones, and spacing them out makes the excellent ones work harder.

This is Apple finally treating TV+ like a service instead of a side project, which only took them five years.

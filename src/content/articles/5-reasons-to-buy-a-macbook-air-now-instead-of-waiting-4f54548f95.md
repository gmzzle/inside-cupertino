[MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/07/28/5-reasons-buy-macbook-air-now-no-waiting/) published a list of reasons to buy the M5 MacBook Air right now instead of waiting for the M6 model. Some of the reasoning holds up. Some of it doesn't.

The solid part: yes, the M5 Air at $1,199 on Amazon (down from the new $1,299 MSRP) is genuinely good value. The 16GB base RAM fixed the biggest problem with previous Airs, and the M5 chip is more than fast enough for the overwhelming majority of what people actually do on laptops. The $699 MacBook Neo isn't real competition here - it's an iPad replacement that happens to run macOS, not a proper ultraportable. If you need a new laptop today and your budget is around $1,200, the Air makes sense.

The wobbly part: the idea that prices will definitely keep climbing because of the memory shortage. Apple raised prices in June, sure, and the shortage is real. But memory pricing is cyclical, not linear. Every shortage eventually breaks. NAND and DRAM prices have spiked and crashed half a dozen times in the past fifteen years. Betting that component costs will stay elevated through an entire product cycle - and that Apple will pass those costs along again rather than eat some margin - is not the lock MacRumors presents it as.

More to the point, Apple has other levers. They could hold pricing and cut costs elsewhere. They could absorb some of the increase to keep the Air positioned where it needs to be. The M5 launched at $1,099 for years before this hike. Apple knows what the Air's job is: high-volume workhorse that brings people into the ecosystem. Pricing it into MacBook Pro territory defeats that purpose.

If you need a laptop now, buy now. Waiting for theoretical future savings when your current machine is limping along is pointless. But if your MacBook still works fine and you're just shopping early, don't let fear of higher prices rush you into a purchase six months ahead of schedule.

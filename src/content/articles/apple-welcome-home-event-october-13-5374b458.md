Apple made it official this morning. Marketing chief Greg Joswiak posted a short teaser with four words, "Welcome home. October 13.", and press invites went out at the same time for an "Apple Experience" in Tribeca, New York, at 9:00 a.m. ET (6:00 a.m. PT) next Tuesday, according to [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/08/apple-announces-special-event-for-october-13-welcome-home/) and [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/08/apple-product-launch-october-13/).

The tagline does most of the work. This is the launch Apple has been circling for more than a year: its first real attempt to own the living room since HomePod.

## What Apple actually said

Very little, which is normal. Apple has confirmed only the date, the "Welcome home" line and the New York hands-on session. It hasn't said whether there will be a full keynote-style video or just a batch of Newsroom posts.

Bloomberg's Mark Gurman said on his Power On podcast today that Apple is planning an "online home event," [per MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/08/new-apple-product-still-expected-october-13/). Whatever the format, reporters and creators in Tribeca should get hands-on time with the hardware the same day.

## What's expected

Three products keep showing up in every report:

- **A smart home hub.** This is the new one. It has been described as a cross between an iPad and a HomePod, with a roughly 6-inch square display, voice as the main way to use it, and Siri AI built in. Reports point to two versions, one that mounts on a wall and one that sits on a half-dome speaker base. A camera and a presence sensor are said to recognize who walked into the room and show that person's own widgets, calendar and apps. MacRumors puts the likely price around $350.
- **A new HomePod mini.** New colors, Apple's N1 wireless chip for better Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, and a faster processor so it can run Siri AI.
- **A new Apple TV 4K.** Same outside, faster inside: an A19-class chip, more RAM, the N1 chip and an updated Siri Remote. MacRumors contributor Aaron Perris found an Apple image file labeled "Apple TV 4K (4th generation)," which is about as close to confirmation as a leak gets.

We also [reported yesterday](/articles/apple-lg-smart-home-doorbell-lock-a25dd84a/) on the LG-branded doorbell, deadbolt, thermostat and cameras that Bloomberg says Apple helped design. The Verge and 9to5Mac both expect those to be unveiled next week too, even if, as reported earlier this week, they don't go on sale until a few months after Apple's own hardware.

## What probably isn't coming Tuesday

An OLED iPad mini is close, but MacRumors' sources say Apple plans a separate event in the last week of October, and the tablet could land there instead. The touchscreen OLED MacBook Pro is expected later this month and more Macs before the end of the year, but neither is tied to the home launch. Don't go in expecting a Mac on Tuesday.

## Why it took this long

The hub was reportedly meant to ship in 2025. What held it up wasn't the hardware. It was Siri. A screen in the kitchen that you mostly talk to is only as good as the assistant behind it, and the old Siri wasn't good enough to carry a new product category. Siri AI only shipped last month, and it still carries a beta label, as [The Verge noted](https://www.theverge.com/news/1008039/apple-smart-home-event-october-13th).

So Tuesday is less about three gadgets than about whether Siri AI is ready to be the main way people use an Apple device. On an iPhone, a bad Siri answer is an annoyance; you tap your way around it. On a wall-mounted hub with no keyboard, it's the whole product.

## What to watch

Three things will tell you whether this is a real push or a soft launch. First, price: around $350 puts the hub well above the cheapest smart displays from Amazon and Google. Second, whether Apple keeps Siri AI's beta tag on a product built around it. Third, the LG gear: if those accessories show up with dates and prices, Apple is building a full home ecosystem, not just selling a nicer speaker.

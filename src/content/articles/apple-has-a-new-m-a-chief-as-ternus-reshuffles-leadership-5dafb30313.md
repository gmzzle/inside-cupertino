[MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/08/apple-acquisitions-new-lead/) reports that Steve Smith is taking over Apple's mergers and acquisitions team from Adrian Perica, who's held the role since 2013. That's a long run for one of Apple's quieter but more consequential jobs - the M&A team handles everything from acquiring small AI startups to structuring the ChatGPT deal that put OpenAI inside Siri.

But here's the telling part: Perica reported directly to Tim Cook. Smith reports to CFO Kevan Parekh. That's not a lateral move for the function - that's M&A getting bumped down the org chart. Moving dealmaking back into finance suggests Ternus sees it as more operational than strategic, or at least less important than it was under Cook's watch. Cook loved acquisitions. He bought a company every three weeks on average for years. Ternus might not.

Perica isn't getting fired - he's staying on to run iCloud, Fitness+, and Apple News, a portfolio he's managed since 2023 alongside M&A. Now he reports to Eddy Cue, who's consolidating power as the services empire builder. Cue just absorbed the App Store after Phil Schiller stepped down in August, and he's reportedly eyeing succession candidates. Perica's one of them. So is Oliver Schusser, who runs Apple Music. If you're keeping score, Cue is 60, Perica is in his early 50s, and Schusser is 51. Cue's building his bench.

The subtext here is that Ternus is reshuffling the deck Cook left him. Cook ran Apple as a federation - powerful lieutenants with direct reporting lines and long tenures. Ternus is reorganizing around functions, not personalities. M&A goes to finance. Services consolidate under Cue. It's early, but this looks like Ternus cleaning up the structure to match how he wants to run things, not how Cook did.

Smith's appointment matters less than the reporting line. Apple will keep buying companies, but the guy making the calls now sits two layers down from the CEO instead of one.

Apple added eight new products to its online store this week, and seven of them are from Aqara. [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/02/apple-now-sells-aqara-adaptive-temperature-thermostat-and-seven-more-accessories/) caught the update, which includes the Aqara Thermostat Hub W200—a $199 adaptive thermostat that competes directly with Nest and Ecobee.

This isn't subtle. Apple's third-party accessory store has always been selective, which makes stocking seven products from one manufacturer a clear vote of confidence. Aqara runs on Matter, Apple's preferred smart home standard, and the company's stuff actually works. The thermostat learns your schedule, supports geofencing, and integrates with HomeKit without the usual parade of firmware updates and troubleshooting Reddit threads. It's the kind of product Apple wants people to associate with its smart home push because it doesn't make Apple look bad.

The timing matters. Google just killed off Works with Nest integrations last year, pushing everyone toward Matter whether they wanted to or not. Ecobee's latest models are fine but haven't moved the needle in years. Apple clearly sees an opening for a third option that doesn't come with Google's baggage or Ecobee's stagnation. Aqara's also cheaper than both incumbents while offering most of the same features, which helps when you're trying to convince someone that HomeKit is worth the trouble.

The other seven products—cameras, sensors, door locks—fill gaps in Apple's own lineup without Apple having to build them. Apple tried making a router and gave up. It's never going to make a smart lock. But it can curate a collection that feels cohesive enough to compete with Amazon's endless landfill of white-label Zigbee gadgets. Aqara's aesthetic doesn't hurt either. Clean, minimal, the kind of thing you wouldn't mind mounting on a visible wall.

This is Apple's smart home strategy now: pick winners, promote them hard, hope Matter delivers on its interoperability promise so people stop blaming Apple when things don't pair. Whether that's enough to catch up to Google and Amazon is another question, but at least the products are good.

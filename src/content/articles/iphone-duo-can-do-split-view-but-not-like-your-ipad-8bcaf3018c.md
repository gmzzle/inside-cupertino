The iPhone Duo does split-screen multitasking, but it's locked to a 50/50 split. No dragging the divider to give Mail more room while keeping Messages visible. No three-quarter layouts. Just an even split down the middle, [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/11/iphone-duo-split-view-not-like-ipad/) reported, and it's "likely to be a disappointment" for anyone used to the iPad's flexibility.

Except Apple isn't being lazy here. The limitation is deliberate, and it's actually the smartest way to handle the Duo's unusual display geometry. The inner 7.6-inch screen has a 1:1.4 aspect ratio, and when you split it down the crease, each half is roughly the same size and shape as the outer cover display. That means every app already has to work in that dimension. A 50/50 split gives both apps a canvas they've already optimized for. Start letting users drag that divider around and you're asking developers to support arbitrary aspect ratios on a device that also has a literal hinge running through it.

The iPad has had resizable Split View since iOS 9 in 2015, but the iPad doesn't fold in half. Its multitasking model evolved over a decade on a flat piece of glass with predictable dimensions. The Duo is a different beast. It's not just a bigger iPhone - it's a device that switches between a phone and a small tablet depending on whether it's open or closed. Asking it to behave exactly like an iPad ignores the constraints that come with a hinge and two separate screens.

This is Apple choosing consistency over flexibility, and for a first-generation foldable, that's probably the right call. Developers get a simpler target. Users get a multitasking experience that actually works instead of one that technically supports every configuration but looks broken in half of them. The trade-off is real, but so is the reasoning. The Duo isn't an iPad that folds - it's an iPhone that unfolds, and the software reflects that.

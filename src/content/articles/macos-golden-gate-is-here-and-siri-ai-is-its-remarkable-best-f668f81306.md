[AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/09/14/macos-golden-gate-is-here-and-siri-ai-is-its-remarkable-best?utm_source=rss) says Siri AI in macOS Golden Gate is "mostly fantastic," which is basically Apple-watcher code for "it finally doesn't suck." After years of Siri on Mac being the feature you accidentally triggered and then immediately dismissed, this is progress.

The timing here matters. Apple's been on the AI hype train hard since WWDC 2024, promising smarter assistants and tighter integration. Golden Gate is where that pitch has to start delivering. If Siri still felt like a bolt-on voice search that takes three seconds to wake up and can't remember context across requests, the whole Apple Intelligence narrative falls apart. So yeah, Siri working well on macOS isn't just nice—it's load-bearing.

What's interesting is that Siri has always been *fine* on iPhone. Not great, but functional enough that people use it for timers and texts. On Mac, though? It's been a ghost. The menu bar icon was easy to ignore, the responses were slow, and nobody wanted to talk to their laptop in an office anyway. If Apple actually made it fast and contextually aware—like, remembering what you just asked or pulling from your open documents—that changes the calculus. Suddenly it's a productivity tool, not a parlor trick.

AppleInsider does note "a few aspects that are still startlingly poor," which is the kind of hedge that makes you wonder what didn't make the cut. My guess: Siri still can't do complex multi-step requests, still gets confused by ambiguous phrasing, and probably still can't interact deeply with third-party apps. Those gaps have been Siri's problem since 2011, and one OS release won't fix that. But if it's faster and smarter for the stuff people actually ask it to do—file searches, calendar checks, quick calculations—that's enough.

Golden Gate is also the first macOS to ship after Apple's AI pivot became the company line. If Siri here is legitimately better, it shows Apple can execute on the promise. If it's just incrementally less bad, that's a different story.

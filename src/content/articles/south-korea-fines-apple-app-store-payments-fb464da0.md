While everyone's eyes are on Apple's touchscreen MacBook event in Los Angeles today, a regulator in Seoul handed down a decision that matters more to the App Store business. South Korea's Korea Media and Communications Commission (KMCC) fined Apple **20.81 billion won** and Google **42.9 billion won**, a combined 63.7 billion won, for how the two companies run payments in their app stores, [Yonhap reports](https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20261028010000320).

In U.S. dollars, Apple's share works out to roughly $15 million. Outlets are using slightly different exchange rates, so you'll see the combined total quoted anywhere from about $45 million ([Yonhap](https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20261028010000320)) to $47.6 million ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/korea-google-apple-app-tech-fine-aedd6061a785441380d4daaf7afbc43d)).

## What the regulator found

South Korea passed a law in 2021 meant to stop app stores from forcing developers onto their own in-app payment systems. The commission says both companies followed the letter of it but not the spirit. According to the [Seoul Economic Daily's account](https://en.sedaily.com/technology/2026/10/28/korea-fines-google-429-billion-won-apple-2081-billion-won) of the ruling:

- **Third-party payments came with a 26% commission.** Developers could use an outside payment processor, but they still owed the store a 26% cut, plus their own processing fees and costs. The commission concluded that left little real reason to pick anything but Apple's or Google's system.
- **Apple made developers submit a separate app** to use third-party payments, which the regulator counted as another obstacle.
- **App reviews stalled without explanation.** Both companies were cited for delaying reviews without giving developers specific reasons or a timeline when asked.
- **Apple calculated its commission differently for Korean developers.** From July 2015 to January 2023, the regulator says Apple took its 30% from Korean developers' VAT-inclusive prices while taking it from pre-tax amounts for overseas developers. The commission estimates that brought Apple about 149.6 billion won in extra revenue. Apple argued the gap came from changes to Korea's VAT law.

The commission added 20% to both fines because the payment practices continued during the investigation. It then cut Apple's by 20% because Apple fixed the VAT commission issue on its own. No criminal complaints will be filed, [Yonhap says](https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20261028010000320).

## What happens next

On top of the fines, both companies have to stop the practices, change their payment policies and review procedures, publicly disclose the orders, and file plans and progress reports showing how they're complying. The regulator plans to formally notify them in November.

Apple said it disagrees with the decision and is weighing its options under Korean law, according to [CNA](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/business/south-korea-media-commission-fines-google-and-apple-over-app-payment-systems-6499616). It also said it has worked with outside developers to support innovation on the App Store.

## Why it matters

In money terms, $15 million is pocket change for Apple. The orders matter more than the fine. This case started with a notice in October 2023, and the commission took three years to finish it, partly because of vacancies on the commission. If Seoul now makes Apple lower that 26% rate or stop treating outside payments as second-class, Korea joins the U.S., the EU and Japan, which have all pushed Apple to loosen its payment rules in the last few years.

For developers, the review finding may matter just as much as the fees. Being told to explain delays and give timelines could change how App Review handles apps in Korea, and an unexplained hold is one of the most common complaints developers have everywhere.

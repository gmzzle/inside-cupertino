One of the biggest complaints about the iPhone Duo is that Apple's foldable makes you go back to a fingerprint. The Duo uses a Touch ID power button instead of Face ID, and after years of unlocking an iPhone just by looking at it, a lot of owners have noticed.

The second iOS 27.3 beta gives them something closer to the old habit. MacRumors, which highlighted the change in its [weekly Top Stories roundup](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/21/top-stories-iphone-duo-face-unlock/) today, calls the new face unlock option "in some ways a stopgap." That's fair. It's also a pretty careful one, and the limits Apple put around it say more than the feature itself.

## What it actually does

The setting is called **Unlock with Front Camera**, and it lives under Touch ID & Passcode in the Settings app. Based on [MacRumors' rundown of beta 2](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/16/ios-27-3-beta-2-everything-new/) and its [hands-on](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/16/iphone-duo-face-unlock-hands-on/), here's how it behaves:

- **It only works when the Duo is closed.** It uses the camera above the outer screen and only at the Lock Screen. Open the phone and you're back to Touch ID or your passcode.
- **It needs a recent Touch ID or passcode unlock first.** After about 45 minutes of inactivity, you have to use Touch ID or your passcode again before face unlock works.
- **It only gets you past the Lock Screen.** Apple Pay, saved passwords and logins, and other sensitive actions still need Touch ID.
- **It needs light.** It's an ordinary camera, so MacRumors found it works fairly well in decent lighting but struggles in a dark room.

Apple's description in Settings, as quoted by [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/11/16/apples-face-unlock-for-iphone-duo-works-well-avoids-androids-issues), says the feature works "along with a recent Touch ID or passcode authentication" and that "Face recognition data never leaves your device."

The same beta also adds **Close to Lock**, under Display & Brightness, which locks the Duo when you fold it instead of moving what you were doing to the outer screen.

## Why Apple fenced it in

This is the interesting part. Face ID uses dedicated depth-sensing hardware to map your face in 3D. The Duo doesn't have it. This feature matches your face with a regular camera, and Apple isn't pretending otherwise. According to [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2318489/iphone-duo-gets-face-unlock-with-the-latest-ios-27-beta/), the setup screen warns that the feature "provides convenience" but "isn't as secure as Touch ID or Face ID."

Once you accept that, every limit makes sense:

- **The 45-minute window** means a strong check, your fingerprint or passcode, always happened recently. Face unlock just stretches a session you already proved was yours. A phone left in a drawer overnight, or picked up by a stranger hours later, falls back to Touch ID.
- **The Lock Screen-only rule** keeps the weaker check away from things that can cost you money or expose your accounts. Seeing your notifications is one thing. Paying for something or filling in your bank password is another.
- **Closed-only** probably comes down to camera quality. MacRumors guesses the inner under-display camera isn't good enough, and Engadget points to its low resolution. Either way, Apple chose to offer the feature only where it could make it work well.

Early testing is encouraging. Engadget found it unlocked faster than Touch ID and wasn't fooled by a photo of the user's face. AppleInsider couldn't trick it with photos or a video either. Those are a couple of beta tests, not a security audit, but they suggest this isn't the easily fooled photo unlock that iPhone fans have mocked on Android phones for years.

## What owners should expect

Think of it as a shortcut for the times you pick up the phone closed and glance at it, not a Face ID replacement. In practice:

1. **You'll still use Touch ID several times a day.** First thing in the morning, after a long meeting, and whenever you pay or sign in to something.
2. **It's optional, so decide whether it fits you.** If your phone is regularly near people you don't fully trust, like a curious kid or a crowded office, the extra convenience may not be worth it. Touch ID alone is still the more secure setup.
3. **Don't install a beta on your main phone just for this.** It's in the developer beta now. MacRumors says it will reach everyone when iOS 27.3 ships in December.

## What it signals for Duo 2

Apple added face recognition to a phone it originally shipped without it. That tells you it heard the complaint. But it built a limited, software-only version rather than waiting, which tells you real Face ID isn't coming to the hardware people already own.

MacRumors says true Face ID is one of Apple's highest priorities for the second-generation model. There's a catch, though. In a [separate report](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/15/apple-aims-to-upgrade-iphone-duo-in-four-ways/), MacRumors relays Bloomberg's Mark Gurman saying Apple has built Duo 2 prototypes with Face ID on the outer display but is "running out of time" to finish it for that model, so it could slip to the Duo 3.

That's why this stopgap matters more than it might seem. If Face ID does slip, front-camera unlock could be how Duo owners unlock their phones for a couple of generations, not a few months. If you're deciding whether to buy now or wait, treat Face ID on Duo 2 as likely but not guaranteed. For people who already own one, iOS 27.3 makes the phone a little easier to live with. Apple just isn't trusting this face check with anything that really matters yet.

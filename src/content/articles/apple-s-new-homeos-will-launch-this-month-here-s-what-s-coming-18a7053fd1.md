Apple is splitting its smart home platform off from tvOS and giving it a new name: homeOS. [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/02/apples-new-homeos-will-launch-this-month-heres-whats-coming/) reports the rebrand launches October 13 alongside new Home hardware, ending the awkward era where HomePods and Apple TV boxes technically ran the same OS despite having almost nothing in common.

This has been overdue since the HomePod launched in 2018. Bundling smart home devices under tvOS made some backend sense—both used A-series chips, both needed Siri, both sat in living rooms—but it made zero marketing sense. Explaining to normal people that their speaker runs "the Apple TV operating system" was a losing conversation. Now homeOS can stand on its own, which matters if Apple actually plans to ship the rumored smart displays and robot arms everyone keeps whispering about.

The October 13 event date is interesting. That's a Monday hardware launch, which Apple rarely does unless they're confident enough to own the news cycle. It also gives them a full month before the holiday shopping season kicks in, enough runway to get reviews out and stock into stores. Whatever they're announcing, it's not a minor refresh.

The real question is whether homeOS signals a genuine platform investment or just a rebranding exercise. Apple's smart home efforts have always felt like a side project compared to the resources poured into iPhone or even Vision Pro. A dedicated OS could mean dedicated resources, dedicated developer tools, maybe even an App Store that makes sense for thermostats and light switches. Or it could mean the same tvOS foundation with a fresh coat of paint and a new boot screen.

If Apple's serious about homeOS, they need more than a name change—they need hardware that normal people actually want to buy and prices that don't make Google Nest look like the budget-friendly option. October 13 will show us which one they picked.

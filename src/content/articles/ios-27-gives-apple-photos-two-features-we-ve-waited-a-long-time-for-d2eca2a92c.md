[9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/13/ios-27-gives-apple-photos-two-features-weve-waited-a-long-time-for/) reports that iOS 27 is bringing full-featured slideshow creation and upgraded iCloud Shared Albums to the Photos app. Both are overdue enough that calling them "long-awaited" is charitable.

The slideshow thing is embarrassing timing-wise. Apple removed the decent slideshow editor from Photos years ago, replacing it with Memories—algorithmic montages that look slick but give you almost zero control. Great if the AI nails your vacation vibe, useless if you want to make a deliberate presentation for your kid's birthday or a client pitch. Meanwhile, people have been using Keynote or paying for third-party apps to do what iPhoto handled fine in 2012. Bringing back manual slideshow tools isn't innovation; it's Apple admitting the AI-only approach left a gap.

The iCloud Shared Albums upgrade matters more than it sounds. Shared Albums have been stuck in a weird limbo since their debut—useful for family photo pools, but crippled by a 5400-photo limit, no video longer than five minutes, and compressed quality that makes your iPhone 16 Pro shots look like they came from a flip phone. If iOS 27 is lifting those caps and preserving full resolution, it turns Shared Albums from a compromised workaround into something you'd actually choose over a Google Photos shared library. That's a real quality-of-life win for families and group trips.

What's notable is the pattern. Apple spent years pushing AI curation and algorithmic shortcuts in Photos, then gradually realized people still want manual controls for the stuff that matters to them. We saw it with the ability to customize Memories, then better search filters, now this. Turns out "the computer decides" isn't always the move, even at Apple.

These aren't flashy features, but they're the kind that make iOS feel less like it's fighting you.

For a few hours on Friday, the same day the iPhone Duo went on sale, Apple was once again the most valuable public company in the world.

Shares opened higher and hit a record of about $351.73. According to [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4656580-apple-tops-nvidia-to-become-worlds-most-valuable-company) and a [Stocktwits report on Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/apple-overtakes-nvidia-worlds-most-140916277.html) citing Google Finance, that put Apple's market value a little above $5.1 trillion, just ahead of Nvidia's.

It didn't last. Apple drifted lower through the day and closed at $347.73, down 0.14%. By [CNBC's](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/23/apple-nvidia-market-cap-earnings.html) count, Apple ended the day worth about $5.05 trillion and Nvidia about $5.10 trillion. Nvidia keeps the crown for now, but only by roughly $50 billion. That's a rounding error at this scale.

## Why the gap closed

Two things have been moving in opposite directions this month.

- **Apple is up.** CNBC says Apple shares have gained about 4% in October and hit all-time highs this week. Stocktwits puts Apple's year-to-date gain at more than 29%, the best among the so-called Magnificent Seven.
- **Nvidia is down.** CNBC says Nvidia has fallen about 7% this month as investors worry about how the AI build-out is being financed.

This has happened before. Apple briefly passed Nvidia [in July](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/17/apple-reclaims-most-valuable-company-title-from-nvidia-as-it-barrels-toward-5t/) and then fell back. The difference now is the timing: Apple's next catalyst is about a week away.

## The Duo is the story, but it isn't in the numbers yet

The rally came as Apple's first foldable reached stores, with lines and thin stock around the world. Apple's marketing chief Greg Joswiak told CNBC that pre-orders were exceptionally strong, according to the Stocktwits report. He also pushed back on reports that production trouble is behind the long waits, calling it a normal production ramp.

How many Duos Apple can actually build is still unclear. CNBC cited Morgan Stanley supply-chain checks pointing to about 6.5 million units in the second half of 2026. That's small next to the iPhone 18 Pro, but with prices from $1,999 to $3,199, each unit brings in a lot of revenue. This week DigiTimes reported a much higher 12-month target of 14 million, which [we covered Friday](/articles/iphone-duo-launch-day-rationed-14-million-target-7c4e1d92/).

The catch for investors: Apple reports fiscal fourth-quarter results on **Monday, November 2**, and the Duo isn't in them. As [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/06/apple-q4-2026-earnings-nov-2/) notes, that quarter ran from June 28 to September 26. It covers only launch-week iPhone 18 Pro sales, plus the new Apple Watch and AirPods models.

## What to watch on November 2

- **The headline number.** On the last call, Apple guided for 9% to 11% revenue growth in the September quarter. Against last year's $102.5 billion, MacRumors works that out to roughly $111.7 to $113.7 billion. CNBC puts expectations around $113 billion.
- **The holiday-quarter outlook.** This is where the Duo shows up. Any hint about how much supply Apple expects to have, and how much it can sell before Christmas, will matter more than the quarter that already ended.
- **Memory costs and supply limits.** Apple had already warned that supply constraints and currency swings would weigh on iPhone revenue. Higher memory prices across the industry are another pressure on margins.
- **A new voice.** It's John Ternus's first earnings call as CEO. He'll host it with CFO Kevan Parekh at 2:00 p.m. Pacific, per [Apple's investor relations page](https://investor.apple.com/investor-relations/event-details/2026/FY-26-Fourth-Quarter-Results/default.aspx).
- **Services.** CNBC notes services made about $109 billion in fiscal 2025, around a quarter of revenue. With gross margins near 75%, it brings in more than 40% of Apple's gross profit.

## What this means

The "most valuable company" title is mostly bragging rights, and it can change hands on a single good or bad day for chip stocks. Still, it tells you something about the mood: investors are rewarding Apple again for selling hardware that people will line up for, at prices that would have seemed absurd for a phone a few years ago.

Apple also has a busy week before earnings. Its "Pro and tell" Mac launch is on October 28, and iOS 27.2 looks close to release. So the next two weeks will show whether Friday was a blip or the start of a lasting lead. Either way, the Duo's real test on Wall Street comes in January, when Apple reports the holiday quarter. That will be the first one with Duo sales in it.

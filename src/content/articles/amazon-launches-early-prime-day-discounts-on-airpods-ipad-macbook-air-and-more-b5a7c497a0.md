[MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/04/amazon-early-prime-day-apple/) spotted Amazon kicking off early Prime Day discounts on Apple products, and the AirPods Pro 3 at $179 – down from $249 – is legitimately the best price we've seen since June. That's $70 off, which for Apple headphones means Amazon is actually trying.

The move makes sense if you think about Amazon's calendar. October Prime Day exists to grab holiday shopping dollars before anyone else does, and Apple products are the anchor items that get people through the door. Amazon knows that if you're buying AirPods in early October, you're probably filling a cart with other stuff too. The real question is whether Apple's okay with its flagship audio product getting this discounted this early in the quarter, but given how AirPods Pro 3 sales have been tracking since the September launch, they probably need the volume.

What's interesting is the timing overlap with Apple's own online store, which has been running its usual non-sale strategy while its retail partners race to the bottom. Best Buy will match these prices within 48 hours – they always do – and then Apple's going to be the only place selling AirPods Pro 3 at full freight. That's fine for people who want to finance through Apple Card, but everyone else is getting a better deal at a retailer that Apple relies on for distribution. It's the same dance every year, and it's why Apple's earnings calls always emphasize services revenue.

The broader selection includes iPads and MacBook Air models, which suggests Amazon secured a decent allocation from Apple's supply chain. The MacBook Air discounts aren't quite as aggressive, but if you're in the market for an M3 Air and you've got Prime anyway, this is probably the window. Waiting for Black Friday is a gamble – retailers are pushing deals earlier every year because consumer spending data says people want to shop in October, not the week of Thanksgiving.

If you're wondering whether these deals mean Apple's feeling pressure, the answer is probably not. This is just how consumer electronics distribution works now. Amazon gets its margin cut, Apple moves units, and everyone pretends the $249 list price ever meant anything.

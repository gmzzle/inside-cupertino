Apple just launched the Beats 360 over-ear headphones, and [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/22/beats-360-ear-cushions-special-pricing/) noticed something interesting: replacement ear cushions are $9.99 through October, then jump to $49.99 in November. You can grab up to three kits per order in either Performance Knit or UltraPlush materials, each with a matching headband.

This isn't a sale. It's Apple pre-loading your drawer with replacement parts so you'll think twice before upgrading next year. They want you wearing these Beats in 2030, and selling cushions at near-cost for the first month is cheaper than dealing with the PR hit when everyone complains about worn-out pads two years in.

The $50 regular price is genuinely steep for what amounts to fabric and foam, but it's consistent with Apple's accessory pricing across the board. AirPods Max cushions are $69. What's different here is the launch window discount actually makes the math work - $10 cushions every two years is reasonable maintenance cost for $300+ headphones. At $50 a pop, people just buy new headphones instead.

Beats has always been Apple's place to try ideas that would feel weird under the main brand. Selling neon green headphones? Beats. Sponsoring athletes? Beats. Openly admitting that parts wear out and should be replaceable? Apparently also Beats. The fact that this cushion program exists at all puts the Beats 360 ahead of most consumer headphones, where you're supposed to throw the whole thing away when the pads deteriorate.

The three-kit purchase limit is the tell that Apple expects this $10 price to be popular. That's six years of cushion replacements if you swap them annually, or three full refresh cycles if you're hard on gear. Either way, they're banking on you staying in the ecosystem long enough to pay full price eventually - or just buying another pair of Beats because you've already invested in the accessories.

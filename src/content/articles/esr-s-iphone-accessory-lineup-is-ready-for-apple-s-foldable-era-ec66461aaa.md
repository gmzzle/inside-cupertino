Apple announced the iPhone Duo on Tuesday. ESR is shipping cases for it today. That timeline tells you everything about how the accessory ecosystem works now.

The Duo is Apple's first foldable iPhone, which means it's the first time case makers have had to design around a hinge, account for different thicknesses when folded versus open, and figure out screen protection without adding bulk. [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/12/esrs-iphone-accessory-lineup-ready-apples-foldable-era/) notes ESR has "an entirely new category" ready alongside its usual iPhone 18 and 18 Pro lineup. That's not luck. That's Apple sharing specs and CAD files months in advance with select partners under NDA so accessories ship day one.

This matters more for the Duo than any other iPhone launch. Foldables are fragile. Samsung learned this the hard way in 2019 when reviewers broke Galaxy Fold review units within days. The screen is plastic, not glass. The hinge is exposed. You absolutely need a case, and you need one that doesn't interfere with the fold mechanism or add so much thickness that the phone becomes a brick in your pocket. ESR getting this right out of the gate means they've been testing prototypes for months, which means Apple handed them working hardware long before September.

The interesting part is what this says about Apple's confidence in the Duo. They wouldn't seed case makers early if they thought the design might change. They wouldn't let third parties ship accessories on day one if durability was still a question mark. Samsung spent three years iterating foldable designs in public. Apple skipped that. They're launching the Duo like it's a solved problem, and the accessory ecosystem is backing that play.

ESR's lineup also covers the standard iPhone 18 Pro models, which is table stakes at this point. But the Duo accessories are the tell. Apple thinks foldables are ready for regular people now, not just early adopters willing to baby a $2,000 phone.

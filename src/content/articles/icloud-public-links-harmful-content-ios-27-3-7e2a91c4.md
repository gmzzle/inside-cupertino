The iOS 27.3 betas brought Siri AI to every developer and a redesigned Health app. They also brought a quieter change that matters to anyone who shares files from iCloud: Apple will start checking public iCloud links for "harmful content."

Apple spelled it out in a [support document](https://support.apple.com/en-us/149168) published Nov. 3, which [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/04/with-ios-27-3-apple-will-start-checking-icloud-public-links-for-harmful-content/) spotted on Wednesday. Starting with version 27.3 of iOS, iPadOS, macOS and visionOS, anything you share from iCloud with the "Anyone with the Link" option is "made accessible to Apple to check for harmful content."

## What's actually changing

When you share a photo from iCloud Photos or a file from iCloud Drive, you can either send a copy or share a link. A link can go to "People you choose," meaning only people you invite or approve, or to "Anyone with the link." Only that second, public option is affected.

Two things change for public links:

- **Apple checks them.** Apple says items shared with Anyone with the Link will be checked for harmful content in 27.3 and later.
- **They aren't end-to-end encrypted.** Apple says a publicly linked item "is no longer end-to-end encrypted, even if the owner or participants have turned on Advanced Data Protection."

[AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/11/04/iclouds-anyone-with-the-link-option-will-trigger-apple-content-checks) reports that the change doesn't cover "People you choose" sharing, shared albums or content that's simply stored in iCloud. [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/04/ios-27-3-icloud-link-check/) notes that Apple hasn't said whether links created before the 27.3 updates will be checked.

## What happens if something gets flagged

Apple's wording is broad. If you try to upload harmful content, the company says it "reserves the right to move, refuse, modify, remove, or delete the content at any time." It may also block or disable your Apple Account, stop you from creating a new account with the same email address or phone number, "or if necessary, involving law enforcement."

Apple doesn't define "harmful content." MacRumors suggests it could cover malware, malicious files and child sexual abuse material, given the reference to law enforcement. [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1026604/apple-icloud-links-harmful-content-27-3) connects the change to ongoing pressure on Apple to act on abuse material stored in iCloud. Apple itself hasn't said what it's looking for or how the checks work.

## Our read

On the encryption point, this is less of a reversal than it sounds. As 9to5Mac points out, a public link has always been reachable by anyone who has it, including Apple. A file you've published to the open web was never really private. What's new is that Apple now says outright that it will look.

That's a defensible line to draw. Apple is checking content people have chosen to make public, not scanning private libraries on devices, which is the plan that drew heavy criticism in 2021 and that Apple later dropped. Public links are also an easy way to spread malware and abusive material, and other big cloud services already police shared content in their own ways.

The weak spot is the vagueness. "Harmful content" with no definition, combined with penalties that go as far as locking your Apple Account and blocking new ones, is a lot of power with no published rules. Apple's account is the key to people's photos, purchases and devices, so how it handles false positives and appeals matters. Apple should publish what it checks for and how someone can challenge a decision before 27.3 ships.

## What to do

If you share anything sensitive from iCloud, such as tax documents, medical records or family photos, use "People you choose" instead of a public link. That keeps the item limited to the people you invite and, if you use Advanced Data Protection, keeps it end-to-end encrypted. It's also worth going back over anything you've shared publicly and stopping the shares you no longer need.

iOS 27.3 is in developer beta now, and Apple has said the public release will come in December. Watch for whether Apple adds an in-app warning when you pick Anyone with the Link, and whether it explains how flagged content and appeals will be handled.

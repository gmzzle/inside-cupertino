For years the Apple home hub lived in the rumor fog: sketches, codenames, slipped windows, and the eternal "why not just prop up an iPad" dunk. Bloomberg's Mark Gurman just put a calendar on it. **Tuesday, October 13, 2026** is the date Apple is expected to introduce the device — often tagged **HomePad** in the rumor mill — with a design that openly channels the 2002 **iMac G4**, plus a refreshed **HomePod mini** and a new **Apple TV** riding in the same event.

MacRumors and 9to5Mac both walked through the Bloomberg reporting this morning. If the date holds, this is the first time the long-delayed smart-home push stops being a roadmap slide and becomes a Ternus-era product day.

## What Bloomberg actually describes

The hub is a square display roughly **six inches** across, always plugged in over **USB-C**, with no battery and no physical volume or power buttons. Finish is gray aluminum. A **FaceTime** camera sits on the face; speakers and mics live in the base.

Two hardware variants show up in the reporting:

- **Tabletop (J490):** display on a half-dome base via a metal arm you can tilt — the silhouette that makes every Apple fan of a certain age say "lamp" out loud.
- **Wall-mount (J491):** a thinner square base meant to hang, still carrying the audio hardware.

In a multi-hub house the pitch is familiar HomeKit control, FaceTime and intercom, whole-home audio, photo slideshows, and kitchen-facing jobs like timers, recipes, and video — all voice-first. Personalized UI comes from voice plus facial recognition that, per the reporting, is less reliable than Face ID, with iPhone secondary auth as the fallback. Dark rooms get an **Edge Light**; visitors get a mode that hides personal info. The software mix is described as Watch-style apps that open into multi-panel, iPad-like layouts, StandBy-ish clocks and slideshows (including a lava-lamp-style clock), and a first-party set covering Calendar, Messages, Photos, FaceTime, Notes, Reminders, Music, and Podcasts. Siri AI sits in the middle; the OS borrows from iOS, tvOS, and watchOS rather than inventing a fourth religion.

None of that is a quote. It is a paraphrase of what Bloomberg laid out and what MacRumors / 9to5Mac summarized. Treat the October 13 window and the G4 silhouette as the headline until Apple is on stage.

## Why October 13 matters more than the silhouette

The G4 callback will dominate the screenshots. Fine. Nostalgia ships. The bigger story is timing and ownership.

Apple has been late to a screen-on-the-counter category Amazon and Google treated as table stakes for years. HomeKit kept the automation layer; HomePod kept the speaker; Apple TV kept the living-room box. What was missing was a dedicated, always-on face for the home that was not "the iPad you swore you would dock and never did." Putting a date on the hub — and pairing it with the first **HomePod mini** refresh since 2020 (per Reuters / 9to5Mac coverage of the same cycle) and the first **Apple TV** update since 2022 — reads like a coordinated smart-home stack day, not another lonely accessory drop.

It is also the first major expansion of this kind under CEO **John Ternus**. Cook-era Apple talked a patient HomeKit story. Ternus now has to show whether "patient" becomes "ships." October 13 is the first public test of that sentence.

## Yes, we heard the iPad-on-a-stand joke

It will get repeated in every comment thread, so say it once and move on: a six-inch, always-plugged display with a FaceTime camera and a G4 arm is, on paper, adjacent to "just buy a cheap iPad and a stand." The counter-argument Apple will need on stage is not industrial design — the lamp already won the mood-board war. It is software that feels room-native: visitor mode that actually protects privacy, facial personalization that does not become a party trick, multi-hub continuity that does not require a home-networking degree, and Siri that behaves like the kitchen remote people already yell at Echo Shows for.

If the OS is Watch tiles that balloon into iPad panels with a StandBy screensaver when idle, Apple is betting that *dedicated* beats *repurposed*. Repurposed iPads wander upstairs. Dedicated hubs stay plugged in and become infrastructure. That is the Echo Show lesson Apple is finally answering in hardware form.

## What to watch on the 13th

Three checks, in order:

1. **Does the date stick?** Bloomberg set Tuesday, October 13. Event calendars move; treat that as the working window, not a shipping guarantee for every SKU the same day.
2. **How hard does Siri carry?** A HomeKit face without a competent voice layer is a digital photo frame with anxiety. The reporting centers the hub on Siri AI — that better not be marketing wallpaper.
3. **Do HomePod mini and Apple TV feel like companions or leftovers?** A real stack day prices and positions all three together. A hub keynote with "also, speakers and a set-top" buried in the appendix is the old playbook.

Until then: the rumor era has a Tuesday. The G4 arm is the meme. The Ternus question is whether Apple's smart home finally becomes a product line instead of a perpetual beta. October 13 is when we find out which story Apple wants to tell.

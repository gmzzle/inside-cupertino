John Ternus got the headline every new CEO wants. In his first earnings report since taking over from Tim Cook on September 1, [Apple said](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/11/apple-reports-fourth-quarter-results/) it posted $114.5 billion in fiscal fourth-quarter revenue, up 12% from a year ago, with records for September-quarter revenue, iPhone revenue and earnings per share, and an all-time high for Services.

Then the guidance came out, and the stock slid in extended trading, [according to CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/02/apple-aapl-q4-2026-earnings-live-updates.html). The problem wasn't sales. It was what memory chips are doing to Apple's margins.

## The numbers

Against LSEG consensus figures cited by CNBC and [Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/apple-results-beat-wall-street-expectationson-strong-iphone-sales-4992898):

- **Revenue:** $114.48 billion vs. $113.39 billion expected
- **EPS:** $2.10 vs. $1.99 expected. Apple says tariff refunds added 5 cents. Reuters notes Apple still beats without them.
- **iPhone:** $57.58 billion vs. $56.54 billion
- **Mac:** $10.79 billion vs. $9.61 billion, up nearly 24% year over year
- **iPad:** $6.23 billion vs. $6.70 billion, down more than 10%
- **Wearables, Home and Accessories:** $8.30 billion vs. $9.06 billion, down nearly 8%
- **Services:** $31.57 billion, in line with the $31.58 billion estimate
- **Gross margin:** 47.9% vs. 47.5%, including about one point from tariff refunds
- **Greater China:** up 21% to $17.53 billion

For the full fiscal year, [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/02/apple-4q-2026-earnings/) tallies $478.8 billion in revenue and $132.2 billion in net income, both records. Apple also declared a $0.27 dividend, payable November 16.

Remember what isn't in these numbers. The quarter ended September 26, so it includes only about eight days of iPhone 18 Pro sales and none from the iPhone Duo, which went on sale October 23.

## The catch: margins and Services

CFO Kevan Parekh guided December-quarter revenue growth of 7% to 10%. That's above the 6.1% analysts expected, according to CNBC. But he also guided gross margin to 45% to 46%, below the 46.3% consensus and well under this quarter's 47.9%. Services growth is expected to be 8% to 9%.

That Services line matters more than it looks. CNBC says Services grew less than 10% in the September quarter, the slowest pace since 2022. Services is Apple's highest-margin business. Slower growth there, at the same moment component costs are rising, is exactly the combination that squeezes margins.

Ternus didn't dodge the cost issue. "We did see higher memory costs as compared to the June quarter, which was partially offset by the benefit of carrying inventory and lower costs on certain non-memory components," he told Reuters, adding that Apple's price increases "provided some offset." He told CNBC that Apple raised prices "reluctantly." Apple raised iPhone prices by $100 earlier this year as the memory shortage hit, and it raised some MacBook prices too.

## Duo: sold out, with no end date

The Duo wasn't in the quarter, but it dominated the conversation. "Duo is constrained as of today," Ternus [told Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/apple-results-beat-wall-street-expectationson-strong-iphone-sales-4992898). "The ramp is going well, and we're working hard to get it to as many customers as quickly as possible, but at this point, we're not able to predict when supply and demand will be in balance."

On the inner-display problems some owners have reported, Ternus said "a very small percentage" of units have been returned and that Apple is replacing them. [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/02/iphone-duo-shortages-no-end/) notes that Duo orders placed today show delivery estimates in January.

## Siri as a reason to upgrade

Ternus told Reuters the new Siri is now handling 2.5 billion requests a day. Asked on the call how Apple will make money from AI, he pointed first to hardware: Siri "offers a compelling reason for upgrading or switching," he said, per CNBC. He didn't announce a paid AI tier. Apple's capital spending was $2.24 billion for the quarter, CNBC reports, which is tiny next to the AI budgets of the big cloud companies.

## Our take

This was a strong quarter that reads like a warning. Most of the beat came from things Apple has already done, like price increases, a Mac lineup on a roll and strong demand in China. The guidance shows the price of those moves. Memory costs are eating into margins faster than Services can make up the difference, and Services itself is slowing.

The good news for Ternus is that demand isn't the problem. The Duo is supply-limited, not demand-limited, and the revenue guide came in above Wall Street's forecast. The holiday quarter is the real test. It will be the first with Duo sales, the new touchscreen MacBooks and the HomeView hub all counted. If Apple can build enough Duos and keep margins near the guide, today's after-hours dip will look like noise. If the Duo stays stuck at "no end date" through Christmas, the January call will be a lot harder.

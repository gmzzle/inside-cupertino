[9to5Mac](https://9to5toys.com/2026/08/29/sharge-impressive-metal-power-bank-display-review/) is excited about Sharge's new Shargeek 300, a metal power bank with enough ports to charge two MacBooks simultaneously, plus an onboard display and RGB lighting. It also has literal window cutouts in the case so you can admire the circuitry inside, which is either cool or the kind of thing that sounds cool until you're actually carrying it.

The dual-MacBook capability is the real story here. Most people buying a premium power bank want one thing: enough juice to get through a flight or a long day without hunting for outlets. Sharge is betting there's a market for people who want to charge *two* laptops at the same time from a single brick, which feels like solving a problem that doesn't quite exist. If you're traveling with two MacBooks, you're either doing something very specific or you've made some interesting life choices. Either way, you probably have access to a wall outlet.

The design is genuinely striking—metal unibody, see-through windows, an OLED display showing real-time power stats. It's the kind of thing that looks great in product photos and probably gets comments at coffee shops. Whether that's worth the inevitable weight and bulk is another question. Power banks this ambitious tend to be heavy enough that you notice them in a bag, and the Shargeek 300 likely won't break that trend.

Sharge has carved out a niche making power banks that double as desk ornaments, and the Shargeek 300 fits that mold. The RGB lighting is pure excess—nobody needs their battery pack to glow—but it's also the kind of detail that makes a product memorable. If you're the type who cares about how your charger looks on a desk, this will appeal. If you just want something that works and disappears into a bag, this is probably overkill.

The launch deal pricing matters here, because Sharge's previous models have been expensive even by premium accessory standards. A power bank that costs as much as an iPad needs to justify itself, and "it looks cool" only goes so far. The dual-port setup and high wattage output are legitimate selling points for people with actual multi-device charging needs, but the RGB and display feel like features in search of a use case. It's impressive. Whether it's useful is a different conversation entirely.

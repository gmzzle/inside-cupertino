Apple rolled out revised EU App Store fees on Tuesday, simplifying the whole mess down to percentages instead of the Rube Goldberg contraption it had before. The European Commission said it "welcomed" the changes. Epic Games, as reported by [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/08/19/epic-games-disagrees-with-apples-junk-eu-fee-changes?utm_source=rss), called them "junk."

At this point, Epic and Apple exist in a state of mutual opposition so complete that if Apple announced it was shutting down the App Store entirely and giving every developer a pony, Tim Sweeney would find a way to call the ponies "anti-competitive." The new fee structure drops the Core Technology Fee—the per-install charge that made developers sweat over viral free apps—and moves to a straight percentage model. It's cleaner. It's what the EU wanted. Epic still hates it.

The thing is, Epic's complaints aren't entirely without merit. Apple's still taking 10-17% depending on app size and developer tenure, which is better than 30% but still enough to make Sweeney's eye twitch. And the structural reality hasn't changed: Apple controls the platform, sets the terms, and developers either play along or they don't ship on iOS in Europe. Epic wants true sideloading with zero Apple tax. Apple wants to collect rent on its platform. These positions are incompatible.

What's fascinating is how performative this has all become. Epic knows Apple won't capitulate. Apple knows Epic will never be satisfied. The European Commission is the only entity with leverage here, and they've already given Apple's new terms a provisional thumbs-up. That means Epic's real audience isn't Apple or even regulators—it's other developers, public opinion, and maybe future lawmakers in other markets.

The changes go live October 1st, and most developers will probably just shrug and move on because percentages are easier to plan around than per-install fees that could bankrupt you if your app goes unexpectedly viral. Epic, meanwhile, will keep calling everything Apple does "junk" until the heat death of the universe.

The Siri AI developer news took most of the attention from yesterday's iOS 27.3 betas. For a lot of iPhone owners, the change they'll actually notice is the Health app.

Apple's redesigned Health app is back in iOS 27.3 beta 1. It's the same overhaul Apple tested in the iOS 27.2 betas and then [pulled before that update shipped](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/03/ios-27-3-health-app/), so iOS 27.2 shipped on Oct. 26 without the feature many people had been waiting for. This time Apple posted it on its [Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/11/redesigned-health-app-expands-language-support/) and says it will roll out "as a free software update later this year." With iOS 27.3 expected in December, that would make it Apple's last software release of 2026.

## What's actually new

According to Apple, there are three main changes.

- **Insights tab.** This tab pulls together summaries of heart, sleep, readiness, fitness, vitals and cycle tracking data throughout the day. A "For You" section gives recommendations based on your habits. Apple's example is advice on adjusting your wind-down routine after several nights of interrupted sleep. [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/03/ios-27-3-health-app/) says the Vitals section now has separate overnight and daytime views and adds HRV, with both overall and recovery readings. The readiness score shows up for Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4 owners.
- **Longevity tab.** This tab looks at your data over a long stretch of time in seven areas: heart health, sleep, mental wellbeing, movement, metabolic health, hearing and nutrition.
- **At-home movement evaluations.** Using the iPhone camera and an Apple Watch, the app measures flexibility, strength, balance, movement mechanics and VO2 max, with live audio and visual feedback. Apple says this runs entirely on device and that "no video is ever recorded, stored, or shared."

Apple also expanded the languages it supports: global English, Chinese (Simplified and Traditional), French, Japanese, Korean, Norwegian, Portuguese, Spanish, Turkish and Vietnamese. One MacRumors commenter said the new app finally works with UK English.

## Health Age is the headline, for better or worse

The Longevity tab is built around Health Age. It takes Apple Watch data, including VO2 max, resting heart rate, sleep and heart rate variability, and turns it into a number that shows how your body is doing compared with your actual age. You can add lab results such as A1c and LDL cholesterol. MacRumors reports that Apple will offer a $119 lab panel through Quest Diagnostics that feeds into Health Age, and that you can also upload recent lab results or sync medical records. [Techzei](https://techzei.com/apple-health-app-redesign-ios-27-3/) says the Quest panel is US-only.

It's a smart feature, and it will divide people. One number is easier to act on than a dozen charts, and showing VO2 max and resting heart rate as "years" makes abstract numbers feel personal. But wellness estimates presented as an age are easy to read as a diagnosis. One of the top comments on MacRumors joked that next year Apple will add a predicted death date. The joke lands because it captures how the number can feel.

Here's our read. Health Age is a fitness and lifestyle estimate, not a medical test, and you should treat it that way. If your number looks bad, take it as a reason to look at your sleep, how much you move and your bloodwork with a doctor, not as a reason to panic. Apple also wants this number to feel credible, and offering a paid lab panel through the app is a new step for the company.

## Who gets it

The fine print matters here. Apple says the redesigned Health app needs an Apple Intelligence-enabled iPhone or iPad, and some features are iPhone-only. Health Age requires an Apple Watch. The VO2 max test requires an Apple Watch, AirPods Pro 3 or a third-party heart rate sensor. Some features are limited to users 18 and older.

[iDrop News](https://www.idropnews.com/news/redesigned-apple-health-app-returns-ios-27-3-beta-1/270553/) reads Apple's wording as all-or-nothing. If it's right, iPhones without Apple Intelligence, like the iPhone 15 and 15 Plus, stay on the current Health app. Apple hasn't said that outright, but nothing it has published suggests a lighter version for older phones.

## Our take

Apple has collected health data for more than a decade, and the Health app has mostly just stored it. This redesign is the first real attempt to tell you what that data means. That's the right direction, and the on-device design of the movement tests is the kind of privacy choice Apple should be making.

Tying the redesign to Apple Intelligence hardware is still a real cost for owners of older iPhones. Health tools are arguably the most broadly useful thing Apple builds, and here they also work as a reason to upgrade. Apple has also already pulled this feature once. Until iOS 27.3 actually ships, we'd count December as likely, not guaranteed.

The things to watch are whether the app survives the rest of the 27.3 betas, how Apple explains Health Age inside the app, and whether the Quest lab panel expands beyond the US.

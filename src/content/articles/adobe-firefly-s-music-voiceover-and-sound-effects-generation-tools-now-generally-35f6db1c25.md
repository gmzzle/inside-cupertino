Adobe just flipped Firefly's audio tools from beta to general availability, according to [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/20/adobe-fireflys-music-voiceover-and-sound-effects-generation-tools-now-generally-available/). You can now generate background music, voiceover, and sound effects directly in Creative Cloud apps without worrying about copyright strikes.

The timing here isn't subtle. Apple's been shipping AI features that feel half-baked and overly cautious, while Adobe's cranking out tools people will actually use in production work. Need a 30-second upbeat track for a TikTok cut? Done. Need a door slam sound effect without scrolling through Freesound for twenty minutes? Also done. This is the kind of boring-but-essential AI application that saves working creatives actual time, not the parlor trick stuff that demos well on stage.

What Adobe gets right is the licensing piece. Every generated track comes commercially licensed, which means you're not setting up your client for a DMCA takedown six months after delivery. That's not sexy, but it's the difference between a tool professionals can trust and one they'll avoid out of self-preservation. Compare that to the morass of "AI-generated" stock music sites where the rights situation is murky at best.

The elephant in the room is what this means for actual musicians and sound designers. Adobe trained these models on something, and while they claim everything's licensed, the specifics remain conveniently vague. The company's been burned before on this exact issue with their image models. At least they're paying *someone*, which is more than you can say for half the generative AI industry.

For Final Cut Pro users, this tilts the playing field a bit more toward Premiere. Adobe's building a full AI audio production pipeline inside Creative Cloud while Apple's video tools still feel like they're from 2019 with a Metal renderer bolted on.

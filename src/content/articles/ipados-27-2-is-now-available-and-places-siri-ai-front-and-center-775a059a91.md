[AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/10/26/ipados-272-is-now-available-and-places-siri-ai-front-and-center?utm_source=rss) is billing iPadOS 27.2 as putting "Siri AI front and center," which is doing a lot of heavy lifting for what amounts to feature parity with macOS. When you select text with a trackpad or mouse on iPad, you now get an "Ask Siri" popup in the contextual menu. That's it. That's the headline feature.

To be fair, it's genuinely useful if you actually use your iPad with a trackpad—you can highlight something and immediately pipe it to Siri without switching contexts. Apple should have done this ages ago, since the Mac has had it for years and iPadOS has been pushing the "real computer" angle since the Magic Keyboard launched. But framing a long-overdue port as a major AI moment is peak 2026 software marketing.

The other thing conspicuously missing from this update is the Health app redesign Apple previewed back in June. It was supposed to ship in the fall. We're past fall. iPadOS is on its second point release of version 27, and Health is still MIA. Either the redesign hit snags in testing, or Apple is holding it for a bigger marketing beat—maybe alongside new iPads in early 2027. Either way, the silence is louder than the Siri popup.

What's telling is how thin these iPad-specific updates have become. iPadOS used to get its own tentpole features to justify the split from iOS. Now it's getting table scraps from macOS and calling them releases. The platform isn't standing still, but it's not exactly sprinting either.

If you use a Magic Keyboard daily, download the update—the Siri shortcut will save you a few taps. If you don't, there's no reason to rush.

Walmart added Apple Pay to its app and website in October. Now it's gone. [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/11/24/apple-pay-vanishes-from-walmart-app-website-without-explanation?utm_source=rss) reported the quiet removal Tuesday, and Walmart hasn't said a word about why.

This is exactly the kind of move you'd expect from a company that spent twelve years refusing Apple Pay in the first place. Walmart finally caved in August 2026 for in-store purchases, then extended support to digital channels two months later. That lasted all of eight weeks. Apple Pay still works at physical checkout kiosks, but if you're shopping on your phone or laptop, you're back to entering card numbers like it's 2015.

The obvious guess: data. Walmart Pay gives the company a direct line to your purchase history, basket composition, and behavioral patterns that Apple Pay explicitly obscures. When you tap to pay with Apple, Walmart gets a transaction confirmation and nothing else. No item-level linkage to your identity, no cross-session tracking, no algorithmic insights into whether you buy organic or generic. That's the entire point of Apple's privacy model, and it's exactly what makes retailers uncomfortable.

Walmart's grudging acceptance of Apple Pay in stores probably came down to competitive pressure and customer complaints at the register. Online is different. There's no line of annoyed shoppers behind you, no friction penalty for adding an extra step. If Walmart thinks it can nudge people back to saved cards or its own payment flow, it'll try. Whether that calculation holds up against cart abandonment rates is another question.

For now, Walmart's position is clear: Apple Pay in person, where refusing it costs them sales. Everywhere else, they'd rather not.

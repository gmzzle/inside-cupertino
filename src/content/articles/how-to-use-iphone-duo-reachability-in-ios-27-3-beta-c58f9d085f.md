[AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/11/03/how-to-use-iphone-duo-reachability-in-ios-273-beta?utm_source=rss) spotted a welcome addition in the iOS 27.3 developer beta: Reachability now works on the iPhone Duo's internal display. Swipe from either edge and that side of the screen slides over to where your thumb can actually reach it.

This is one of those features that makes you wonder why it wasn't there at launch. The Duo's folded-out display is 8.3 inches corner to corner. Even with two hands, reaching UI elements on the far side means stretching or shifting your grip. Apple shipped Reachability on standard iPhones back in 2014 specifically because the 4.7-inch iPhone 6 was hard to use one-handed. The Duo is nearly twice that size unfolded, and it launched without any equivalent gesture. That's been a real usability gap for a device Apple wants you to treat as your everyday phone when unfolded.

The implementation makes sense. A quick swipe from the left edge brings the left half of the screen to the right side. Swipe from the right, and the reverse happens. It's the same basic idea as the original Reachability, adapted for a screen that's wide instead of tall. Whether you're holding the Duo with your left hand and need to tap something on the right, or vice versa, the feature closes the gap. It's not revolutionary, but it didn't need to be.

What's less clear is why this took until iOS 27.3. The Duo has been out for over a year. Apple had to know from internal testing that one-handed use was awkward. Maybe they thought the market for foldables was still too niche to prioritize. Maybe they assumed everyone would use it two-handed. Either way, shipping a basic accessibility feature in a point-three update feels like playing catch-up on homework they should've turned in months ago.

Better late than never, and if you own a Duo, this will probably become muscle memory within a week.

[MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/08/siri-ai-rumored-to-become-more-agentic/) reported that Apple could make Siri function "more like an agent" as early as next year, according to Mark Gurman. The idea is that Siri would work more like OpenAI's Dots or Meta's Muse—tracking goals, figuring out next steps, doing things without you micromanaging every tap.

This would be a huge shift. Right now, Siri AI is better at understanding what you mean, but it still waits for you to tell it what to do. An actual agent would be different: you say "I need to reschedule my 2pm because I'm stuck in traffic" and it texts the person, finds a new slot on both calendars, updates the meeting, done. That's the theory. Apple has never shipped anything close to that level of autonomy.

The timing matters because Apple is late here. Dots launched earlier this year as a persistent companion that remembers context across days. Muse has been live since spring. Google's been testing agentic features in Gemini. Apple Intelligence barely does app actions yet, and the few it handles are rigid and single-step. Going from that to a proactive agent in 12 months would require either a massive internal leap or a very narrow definition of "agent."

Gurman hedges twice in one sentence—"could" and "as early as"—which tells you Apple's own plans are still soft. This feels like an internal goal that engineering is working toward, not a locked feature for iOS 20 or macOS 16. Apple also has to solve the trust problem: people don't even let Siri send texts without confirmation half the time. An agent that books flights and moves money around needs bulletproof reliability and transparency, two things Siri has never been known for.

If Apple pulls this off by late 2027, it'll be a genuine comeback story. If it's another half-step that requires too much babysitting, it's just Siri AI with a new marketing slide.

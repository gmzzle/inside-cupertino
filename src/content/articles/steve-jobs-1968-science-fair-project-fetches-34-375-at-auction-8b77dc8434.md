[9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/28/steve-jobs-1968-science-fair-project-fetches-34375-at-auction/) reported that Steve Jobs' 1968 science fair project sold for $34,375 at RR Auction this week. It's a hand-built frequency counter from when Jobs was a junior at Homestead High School in Cupertino. The winning bid came in at roughly the price of a base-spec Mac Studio.

The project itself isn't remarkable engineering. Jobs built a digital frequency counter that measured alternating current—competent high school work, but not the kind of thing that would've launched a thousand think pieces if some other kid had submitted it. What makes it worth five figures is the signature at the bottom and what happened afterward. This is the kid who'd cold-call Bill Hewlett at home a year later to ask for parts, then talk his way into a summer job at HP. The frequency counter is less about technical brilliance and more about showing up.

The Jobs memorabilia market has gotten predictably absurd. A signed check from 1976 went for over $100,000 a few years back. His Birkenstock sandals—famously gross, famously auctioned—pulled in $218,750 in 2022. At least those items came from the Apple years, when Jobs was actively building the company that would reshape consumer tech. This science fair project predates Apple by eight years. It's a teenager's homework that happens to have the right name attached.

Still, $34,375 isn't outrageous by collectibles standards. Someone probably thinks it looks good in a display case next to their signed iPhone prototype or whatever. And there's something oddly fitting about a Steve Jobs artifact selling for exactly the cost of premium Apple hardware—like the market found its own exchange rate between past and present. The frequency counter measured alternating current. The auction measured something else entirely.

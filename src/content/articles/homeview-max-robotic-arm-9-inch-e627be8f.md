Apple's $299 HomeView doesn't ship until November 13, and we already know what comes after it.

In this weekend's *Power On* newsletter, Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reports that Apple is working on a higher-end HomeView with a **roughly 9-inch screen** (up from 7 inches) mounted on a **motorized robotic arm that can swivel the display a full 360 degrees**. He expects it in **2027 or 2028**. [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/18/apple-rumored-to-launch-homeview-max/) and [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/18/apples-next-homeview-could-debut-as-soon-as-next-year-with-a-robotic-arm-report/) both picked up the report this morning.

## What Gurman is describing

- **Code name J595.** It reportedly looks like the current HomeView, just bigger and on a moving base.
- **It turns toward you.** Using voice recognition, the device is designed to work out who is talking and rotate the screen to face that person.
- **It sits above HomeView, not in place of it.** Gurman pitches it as a higher-end model in the lineup.
- **No name yet.** Gurman tossed out HomeView Max, HomeView Ultra and HomeView 360 as possibilities. Those are his guesses, not Apple's.

Apple hasn't said anything about the device, and there's no price. 9to5Mac's read is that you shouldn't expect it at $299, which seems safe for a bigger screen with motors in it.

## Why this fixes HomeView's most obvious limit

The HomeView Apple announced on October 13 has a [fixed 7-inch screen](/articles/homeview-apple-tv-299-november-13-homepod-mini-2-304c39ff/). It can recognize up to six people by face and give each a personal Home Screen, but it can't do anything about where it's pointing. Put it in the middle of a kitchen island or a dining table and somebody is always looking at it from the side. Gurman frames the robotic version as Apple's answer to exactly that.

It's also the clearest sign yet that the HomeView we're getting is the first step, not the whole plan. We [weren't sold](/articles/the-macrumors-show-was-homeview-worth-the-wait-ae9ca32af2/) on the $299 model as a must-buy. A screen that follows the conversation around the room is a more interesting idea, if Apple can make it feel helpful instead of creepy.

## This sounds a lot like the 'Pixar Lamp'

If the specs sound familiar, they should. Gurman has been reporting for years on a tabletop robot with a 9-inch, iPad-like display on a thin robotic arm that tilts and spins 360 degrees and turns to face whoever is speaking. According to [MacRumors' summary](https://www.macrumors.com/guide/apple-robot/) of that reporting, some people inside Apple nicknamed it the "Pixar Lamp," and Apple was aiming for 2027 with a target price around $1,000. Bloomberg reported in [August 2024](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-08-14/apple-pushes-ahead-with-tabletop-home-device-in-shift-to-robotics) that several hundred people were working on it.

This morning's report doesn't say J595 is that same robot, or whether the $1,000 target still holds. But a 9-inch screen, a 360-degree arm and voice-tracking in the 2027 window is a very close match. Putting it under the HomeView name would give Apple's robotics work a far more familiar entry point than a brand-new product category.

## Should this change your HomeView plans?

Probably not. A device that's at least a year away, and maybe two, with no price, isn't a reason to skip one that ships next month. If you want a kitchen screen for timers, recipes, cameras and Siri AI this year, the 7-inch HomeView is the only Apple option. Just know it isn't available in the EU, where Apple says Siri AI still isn't offered on iOS.

If you were on the fence anyway, and the fixed screen was part of why, waiting to see whether the swiveling model is real is a reasonable call.

## What to watch

- **HomeView reviews and November 13 deliveries.** How well the face recognition and per-person screens work will say a lot about whether a moving version is worth paying more for.
- **Siri AI on home devices.** A screen that turns toward whoever is talking only makes sense if Siri can keep up with the conversation.
- **More J595 detail.** Price, a firmer date, and whether it's the old tabletop robot under a new name.

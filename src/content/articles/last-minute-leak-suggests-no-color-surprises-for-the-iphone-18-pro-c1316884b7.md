[AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/09/09/last-minute-leak-suggests-no-color-surprises-for-the-iphone-18-pro?utm_source=rss) is running a Bloomberg report minutes before Apple's event that predicts the iPhone 18 Pro color lineup. The headline promises "no surprises," which is probably the most honest thing you'll read about an Apple event all day.

The timing here is ridiculous. Publishing color predictions when the actual phones will be on stage in less time than it takes to brew coffee isn't journalism, it's hedging. If Bloomberg's right, they look prescient. If they're wrong, nobody remembers by lunch because Apple already showed the real thing.

What's actually interesting is that leakers have been fighting about iPhone 18 Pro colors for months, and now everyone's suddenly converging on the same safe picks. That usually means one of two things: either Apple's supply chain finally locked in, or everyone's just repeating the same educated guess and calling it reporting. The Pro lineup's color palette has been pretty conservative since the iPhone 12 Pro dropped those flashy blues and greens for graphite and silver. Apple learned that Pro buyers want understated, and "Burgendy" - however you spell it - fits that mold better than, say, hot pink.

The real story isn't what colors Apple picked. It's that we've reached the point where color leaks qualify as news hours before an event. This is what happens when hardware design has plateated so hard that finish options become the most interesting variable. The iPhone 18 Pro will almost certainly be faster, have better cameras, and cost more. The color is the only drama left.

Apple will announce the actual colors in about twenty minutes, and then we can all move on to arguing about whether titanium still scratches too easily.

[9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/27/deals-macbook-pro-find-my-charger-airpods/) is flagging deals on the M5 Pro MacBook Pro, and the discount structure is... revealing. The 24GB RAM model is $500 off. The 48GB model is $400 off. That's backwards from what you'd expect, and it probably means the 24GB config sold worse than Apple anticipated.

This is the kind of pricing inversion you see when a retailer needs to move specific SKUs. The 48GB model likely appeals to a narrower buyer pool—pros who know they need the headroom and were going to buy it anyway. The 24GB sits in this awkward middle ground: too expensive for casual buyers, not enough RAM for the people who'd actually max it out. So now it's getting the bigger haircut to clear shelves before the M6 generation shows up this fall.

The M5 Pro launched at a weird time anyway. Apple released it in March, which meant anyone who bought during the usual November refresh cycle was only six months into ownership when the new models dropped. That compressed window probably created some buyer hesitation, and now we're seeing the fallout in discount season. July deals this aggressive on MacBook Pros used to be rare—Apple's margin control was tighter. Now it's just another signal that the annual refresh cadence is creating inventory management problems downstream.

For what it's worth, if you actually need a MacBook Pro right now and were eyeing the 24GB model, $500 off is legitimately good. That config usually holds value better than the base models in resale, even if it's currently the one gathering dust in warehouses. Just don't expect this discount to last once Amazon's algorithm decides the inventory problem is solved.

Apple's base iPad has been languishing on the A16 chip since late 2024, making it the only product in the main lineup that can't run Apple Intelligence. The expectation was an A18 refresh sometime this year. Instead, [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/03/apples-next-ipad-a19-details-release-date/) reports Apple's planning to skip right over A18 and ship an A19-powered model.

This isn't Apple being generous. It's Apple fixing a problem they created by letting the entry-level iPad rot for 18 months while every other device got Intelligence support. By the time this thing ships—likely spring 2027—the A18 will be nearly three years old. Shipping it then would mean the cheapest iPad arrives already two generations behind the iPhone. That's a bad look when you're trying to position iPad as the accessible computing device for students and first-time buyers.

The A19 also solves the Intelligence problem cleanly. Apple's made it clear that 8GB of RAM is the floor for on-device AI features, and the A19 will presumably come standard with that. The current $349 iPad has 4GB. Bumping to A19 lets Apple add Intelligence support without the awkward explaining why the "new" A18 model still can't do half of what iOS 18.4 advertises.

Expect the same chassis, the same 10.9-inch display, probably the same $349 starting price. Apple's not redesigning the entry iPad; they're just making sure it can run the software they're about to spend the next three years promoting. The A19 jump is less about performance gains anyone will notice and more about buying another few years before this model becomes the Intelligence Have-Not again.

It's a smart move disguised as spec-sheet leapfrogging—and it only became necessary because Apple let this iPad gather dust for way too long.

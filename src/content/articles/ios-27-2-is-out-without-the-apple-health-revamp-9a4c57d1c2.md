[AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/10/26/ios-272-is-out-without-the-apple-health-revamp?utm_source=rss) reported today that iOS 27.2 is rolling out to the public after four betas, and if you're wondering why Apple skipped 27.1, the answer is peak Apple weirdness: iOS 27.1 exists, but only for the iPhone Duo. The foldable's dual-screen setup apparently required its own version number, so the rest of us just leapfrog straight to 27.2. It's the software equivalent of reserving a table for one person at a restaurant and making everyone else wait outside.

The actual features in 27.2 are thin. FaceTime Dual Capture lets you use front and back cameras simultaneously during calls, which is genuinely useful if you're showing someone something while staying on screen. Apple Music got a new logo—because apparently the old one wasn't music-y enough. That's about it for marquee additions. No complaints about either, but this isn't exactly a feature-packed release.

What's *not* here is more interesting. Apple teased a redesigned Health app back at WWDC, complete with a new interface and better trend tracking. That was supposed to ship sometime in the iOS 27 cycle, and we're now two point releases in with nothing. It's not unusual for Apple to hold features back—iOS 18.2 famously didn't get its headlining AI stuff until December—but the silence around Health is conspicuous. No timeline update, no acknowledgment, just radio silence while the app continues to look like it did in 2020.

The version number skip is functionally meaningless but symbolically annoying. It's a reminder that the iPhone Duo, which Apple positioned as this big dual-screen future, is already fragmenting iOS in small ways. Most users will never notice or care, but it's the kind of organizational quirk that makes you wonder how much internal complexity Apple's willing to tolerate for niche hardware. Dual Capture is nice. A functioning Health redesign would've been nicer.

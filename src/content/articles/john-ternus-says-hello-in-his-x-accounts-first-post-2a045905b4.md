[AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/09/01/john-ternus-says-hello-in-his-x-accounts-first-post?utm_source=rss) reported that John Ternus posted "hello" as his first tweet on X. That's it. One word, lowercase, no punctuation. It's the digital equivalent of walking into a room and nodding.

The choice is deliberate. Steve Jobs started the original iPhone keynote with "hello" in January 2007. Tim Cook opened his own X account—back when it was still Twitter—with the same word in September 2013. Ternus is now the third Apple CEO in a row to use it, which at this point makes it less a tribute and more of a corporate tradition. It's also smart positioning: Ternus gets to signal continuity while still being his own person. He didn't quote Jobs or add a photo of an Apple product. Just the word.

What's more interesting is the timing. Ternus officially became CEO on September 1, 2026, and he set up the account the same day. Cook took two and a half years after becoming CEO to join Twitter. Ternus is moving faster, which tracks with how Apple's been operating lately—less about waiting for perfect moments, more about staying present. The account handle is @johnternus, no cute variations, and the profile is bare-bones for now. No bio, no banner image, just the default egg replaced with what appears to be a placeholder avatar.

Ternus has always been lower-profile than Cook, even as SVP of Hardware Engineering. He did keynote segments, but sparingly. He didn't do earnings calls. He wasn't on Instagram posting Apple Watch selfies. This X account doesn't mean he's suddenly going to become a prolific poster—Cook tweets maybe once a month, and even that feels generous. But it does mean Ternus understands the job requires some level of public presence, even if it's minimal.

The real test isn't the first tweet. It's the second one, whenever that comes.

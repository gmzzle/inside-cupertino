Two weeks ago, Tim Cook told a Shanghai newspaper that Apple Intelligence was approved in China and that a test build was coming "this month." Now Apple has put it in writing, with dates, a feature list, and a partner name.

In a press release on its [China Newsroom](https://www.apple.com.cn/newsroom/2026/10/apple-intelligence-features-are-coming-to-users-in-china/), dated October 31 in China (Friday afternoon in California), Apple said the first Apple Intelligence features for Chinese iPhone users open for testing on **November 4** in the latest iOS developer beta, then reach the public with **iOS 27.3 in December**. Apple didn't give a December day. And Apple confirmed for the first time, in its own words, that **Alibaba's Qwen** will be built into Siri, Writing Tools and Visual Intelligence.

This is the official follow-up to our [October 15 story](/articles/apple-intelligence-china-beta-this-month-siri-ai-left-out-7f899344/), when the timeline was still a mix of Cook's comments and Bloomberg's reporting.

## The dates

- **November 4: testing begins.** It arrives with the next iOS developer beta. [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/30/apple-says-ios-27-3-beta-coming-next-week/) notes that a Wednesday date in China probably means Tuesday, November 3, in the U.S. [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/30/apple-confirms-ios-27-3-beta-is-coming-next-week-with-apple-intelligence-in-china/) adds a caveat: strictly speaking, Apple dated the start of China testing, not necessarily iOS 27.3 beta 1 itself. Either way, Apple rarely announces a beta date at all.
- **December: public release with iOS 27.3.** Bloomberg reported this timing earlier this month. Now Apple has said it too.

One small note: Cook's "later this month" test build didn't happen. The beta now lands in the first week of November, a few days late.

## What Chinese iPhone users get

According to Apple's release, the first wave runs on Apple's own generative models and is largely the original Apple Intelligence feature set:

- **Live Translation** in Messages, FaceTime and Phone, plus in-person translation through supported AirPods
- **Writing Tools** for rewriting, proofreading and summarizing
- **Visual Intelligence** for identifying objects and places through the camera
- **Clean Up** in Photos, plus natural-language photo search and Memory movies made from a text prompt
- **Genmoji**
- **Priority Mail**, Mail summaries and **Smart Reply** in Mail and Messages
- **Notification summaries**, priority notifications and **Type to Siri**

[SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3372831/apple-intelligence-roll-out-iphones-china-december-alibaba-backing) counts roughly 18 features in total.

## Where Qwen fits

Qwen is an add-on, not a replacement for Apple's models. Per Apple, Siri can pass certain requests to Qwen, and users can ask Qwen about photos and documents, including PDFs and presentations. Writing Tools gets a Qwen-powered option for writing original content from scratch. To use it, you sign in with a Qwen account or with Sign in with Apple.

[Jiemian](https://m.jiemian.com/article/15277073.html) (界面新闻) reports that Alibaba says the integration is opt-in, that users decide what to share, and that Alibaba won't use the requests or responses to train its models. That's Alibaba's statement as reported. Apple's release doesn't spell out those terms in the same way.

Notably, Apple's release doesn't mention Baidu. Earlier reports, including Bloomberg's, tied Baidu to search and Siri-related work in China. Whether that work is still coming, has moved, or simply wasn't part of this announcement is an open question.

## What's missing

- **Siri AI.** Apple's release says plainly that this update doesn't include Siri AI or the next-generation Apple Intelligence features shown at WWDC 2026. MacRumors adds that **Image Playground** is also left out. That stands out in a week when Siri AI [added five more languages](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/30/apple-confirms-ios-27-3-beta-is-coming-next-week-with-apple-intelligence-in-china/) elsewhere.
- **iPad, Mac and everything else.** The China launch is iPhone-only. Apple says other platforms will follow "after the necessary approvals," and gives no timeline.

## Which iPhones, and one setting

Supported models are the iPhone 15 Pro and 15 Pro Max, iPhone 16 and later, iPhone Air and iPhone Duo, according to Apple. Both the device language and the Siri language must be set to English or Simplified Chinese.

## Our take

This is a narrow launch, and it's late. Chinese users are getting the 2024-era features roughly two years after the U.S., and the assistant Apple is promoting hardest this fall isn't on the list. Rivals like Huawei and Xiaomi have been shipping their own AI features for a long time.

Still, it's the first time Apple has committed to dates in its own words, after two years of regulatory back-and-forth. Bringing in Qwen also gives Chinese users a capable local chatbot inside the system. For a market as important as China, a working, approved Apple Intelligence in December beats an ambitious one with no date.

## What to watch

- **The November 4 beta.** Watch for whether the full feature list shows up, and how Qwen hand-offs look in Siri.
- **The December date for iOS 27.3.** Apple hasn't named one yet.
- **Baidu.** Whether it shows up in a later update.
- **Siri AI in China.** There's still no public timeline.

Apple shipped a macOS beta with promotional videos for products that don't exist yet. [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/22/top-stories-apple-leaks-camera-airpods/) pulled a clip showing camera-equipped AirPods in action—someone holds up a book, the earbuds see it, Visual Intelligence saves it for later. The kind of demo Apple would normally debut onstage, except this one got bundled into macOS Tahoe 26.7 RC by mistake.

This isn't some supplier leak or CAD render from a case manufacturer. Apple put this in the OS itself. The same build apparently referenced other unannounced hardware too, though the AirPods video is the only one that's surfaced so far. It's the software equivalent of leaving prototype hardware on a barstool, except worse because anyone running the beta could theoretically find it.

Camera AirPods make sense as a concept—Visual Intelligence needs more input methods than just holding up your phone, and earbuds live on your head already. But the execution here is harder than it sounds. Where does the camera sit without making the stems look ridiculous? How much does it chew through battery? And is anyone actually going to use this, or is it another feature that sounds cool in a keynote and gets ignored in practice?

Mark Gurman says they're not launching until next year anyway, which tracks. If Apple were ready to ship these soon, the marketing materials wouldn't still be sitting in beta builds waiting to leak. The company's probably scrambling to figure out whether to acknowledge the leak or pretend it didn't happen and debut the product fresh whenever they're actually ready.

Either way, someone in Cupertino had a very bad week.

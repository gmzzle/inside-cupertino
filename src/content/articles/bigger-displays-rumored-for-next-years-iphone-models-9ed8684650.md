[MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/04/bigger-displays-rumored-for-next-year-iphones/) is reporting that Apple's sampling display panels at 6.4 inches and roughly 7 inches for next year's iPhone 20 lineup. The smaller one would go in the Pro, the larger in the Pro Max. Both would be bigger than what we've got now—the iPhone 17 Pro is 6.3 inches, the Pro Max is 6.9.

This is the third size bump in three years if it happens. The iPhone 16 Pro went from 6.1 to 6.3 inches in 2024. The Pro Max jumped from 6.7 to 6.9. And now we're supposedly headed to 6.4 and 7 inches for the 20th anniversary models in 2027. It's not subtle. Apple used to hold the line on iPhone sizes for years at a time—the 6.1-inch regular Pro stuck around from the iPhone 12 through the iPhone 15. Now it's annual growth, and the trajectory is pretty clear.

The question is whether this is driven by what people actually want or by the need to justify a new phone every year when the software updates are good enough to make last year's model feel fine. Bigger screens help with video and reading, sure. But they also make the phones harder to use one-handed, harder to fit in pockets, and generally less convenient to carry. The iPhone 16 Pro Max is already pushing it for a lot of people. A 7-inch Pro Max starts to sound less like a phone and more like a small iPad that makes calls.

The rumor comes from a Chinese leaker and aligns with earlier reports about the iPhone 20 being a big deal—under-display Face ID, custom camera sensors, haptic buttons, all the anniversary-edition bells and whistles. Bigger displays fit that narrative. But it also fits the narrative that Apple's run out of ways to make the iPhone feel meaningfully new without just making it physically bigger.

If the 6.4 and 7-inch screens happen, they'll be the largest iPhones ever made. That's a milestone, technically. Whether it's one anyone actually asked for is another story.

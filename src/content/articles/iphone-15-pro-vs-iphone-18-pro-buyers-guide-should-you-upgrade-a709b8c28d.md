The [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/guide/iphone-15-pro-vs-18-pro/) buyer's guide lays out the obvious: three years separating the iPhone 15 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro means real changes. Bigger screens with slimmer bezels, all-new cameras at every focal length, dramatically better battery life, and that new variable-aperture Main camera that actually lets you adjust depth of field. On paper, it's a meaningful leap.

But the price tells a different story. The iPhone 18 Pro starts at $1,199 for 256GB—$200 more than the iPhone 15 Pro's launch price. Apple will correctly point out that you're getting double the base storage, so the per-gigabyte math looks better. That's true, and also irrelevant to anyone who remembers when Pro iPhones started at $999. The 18 Pro Max is up $100 over the equivalent 15 Pro Max config. This is Apple testing how much elasticity remains in the premium phone market, and so far, the answer seems to be plenty.

The actual hardware progression is more interesting than the price hike is annoying. The 16 Pro added Camera Control and a 48MP Ultra Wide. The 17 Pro ditched titanium for aluminum unibody, brought vapor chamber cooling, and fixed the front-facing camera that had been stuck at 12MP since 2018. The 18 Pro adds the A20 Pro, shrinks the Dynamic Island, and ships that variable-aperture system that's been rumored for years. All of it matters if you're coming from a 15 Pro. None of it is revolutionary on its own, but stacked across three generations, it's the kind of compounding improvement that makes year-over-year spec sheets look boring and three-year upgrade cycles look smart.

The battery gains deserve emphasis. Significantly longer life plus faster charging means the 18 Pro might actually last a full day under real use without optimization theater. That alone could justify an upgrade for anyone whose 15 Pro battery has started the usual lithium-ion slide into inadequacy.

Apple's bet is that $1,199 doesn't feel expensive if you're spacing out upgrades and the thing you're replacing is three years old and fading. They're probably right, which means $1,199 is the new baseline and we're all just living in it.

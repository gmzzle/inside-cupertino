AT&T just did the carrier thing: acknowledge a mess in a text message, then point at an Apple software update that does not appear to fix it.

Overnight, the carrier texted iPhone 18 Pro Max customers that some phones may fail calls, texts, and data — **including 911** — and told them to install **iOS 27.0.1** immediately via Settings → General → Software Update. That is an emergency-services framing. It is also a handoff. AT&T named Apple’s build. Apple’s **iOS 27.0.1** release notes, for their part, talked about **Face ID restarts** and a **camera color artifact** on 18 Pro hardware. Not cellular. Not AT&T. Not SOS mode.

## What users are actually seeing

Affected **iPhone 18 Pro Max** units on AT&T lose calls, texts, and data — persistently or in waves — and often land in **SOS**. This is not a handful of forum Luddites. Reporting across Apple Support Community, Reddit, and the MacRumors Forums puts the count in at least the **thousands**. Plenty of Pro Max owners on AT&T are fine. That does not make the failure mode less ugly for the ones who are not.

And the update AT&T is pushing? Early reports from people who already installed **27.0.1** — including those who grabbed it when it shipped Monday — say **service did not come back**. Carrier says update. Notes say Face ID. Phones say SOS. Pick your favorite authority.

## The modem split nobody wanted to stress-test this way

Here is the hardware footnote that will not stay a footnote. The **U.S. iPhone 18 Pro Max** is the odd one out of Apple’s new lineup: it ships with a **Qualcomm** cellular modem. The **iPhone 18 Pro**, the **iPhone Duo**, and the **Pro Max outside the U.S.** run Apple’s **C2**. So far the AT&T cellular failures look concentrated on the Qualcomm Pro Max. Correlation is not causation. It is also not something you ignore when one SKU is drowning in SOS and its siblings are not.

Apple has **not publicly commented**. Cause unknown: software, hardware, some cursed intersection of modem firmware and AT&T’s network. If it is software, expect something like **iOS 27.0.2**. If it is silicon or RF, the story gets much more expensive — for Apple, for AT&T, and for anyone who bought the big phone assuming it could call 911.

Until then: AT&T is texting people who may not be able to receive texts, Apple’s changelog is silent on cellular, and a chunk of U.S. Pro Max buyers are staring at SOS. That is not a point release. That is a product trust problem wearing a carrier SMS.

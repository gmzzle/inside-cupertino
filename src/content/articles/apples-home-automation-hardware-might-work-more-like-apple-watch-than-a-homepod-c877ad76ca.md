[AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/08/19/apples-home-automation-hardware-will-work-more-like-an-apple-watch-than-a-homepod?utm_source=rss) dug into macOS Tahoe code and found references to a "Face Gallery" and complications for the long-rumored HomeHub device. Basically, Apple's taking the Watch's customizable face approach and dropping it onto whatever this home automation screen turns out to be.

This is a better call than the HomePod software model everyone expected. The Watch face system works because it's glanceable and personal—you configure it once, then it just surfaces what you need. That's exactly what a countertop or wall-mounted home display should do. Nobody wants to dig through menus to check the thermostat or see who's at the door. Stick a doorbell complication on there, swipe to a calendar face when you need it, done.

The complications framework also means third-party integration should be straightforward. Developers already know how to build Watch complications. If Apple's using the same APIs or something close, apps like Ecobee or Nanoleaf can ship HomeHub widgets without reinventing everything. That matters more than Apple's marketing will admit—this device lives or dies on whether it controls your actual smart home gear, not just Apple's narrow slice of it.

The big question is defaults. Apple Watch faces out of the box are fine but not amazing, and most people never customize them. A home display has different stakes—it's shared by everyone in the house, sitting in a common space, and if the default face is useless, it becomes a $200 clock. Apple needs to nail what ships on that screen day one: weather, calendar, home controls, intercom. Make the default good enough that customization is optional, not required.

If they pull that off, this could be the first home hub that doesn't feel like a science project you're constantly tweaking.

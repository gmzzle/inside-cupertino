If you've been topping up **Apple Cash** without ever handing over your ID, that ends next month. Apple is emailing customers today about updated Apple Cash terms, and the headline change is simple: starting **November 9**, Green Dot Bank, the bank that actually runs Apple Cash, will require identity verification before you can reload your balance at all ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/05/apple-cash-reload-id-check/), [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/05/apple-notifies-apple-cash-users-about-two-upcoming-changes/), [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/10/05/identity-verification-to-be-required-when-reloading-apple-cash)).

## What actually changes

Verification isn't new. What changes is when it kicks in. Until now, Apple Cash only pushed you to verify once your activity crossed a threshold, which MacRumors puts at **$500** in combined adding, sending, and receiving. After November 9, an unverified account can't add money of any amount.

Verification is tied to your account, so for most people it only happens once (Apple notes it may occasionally ask again). You can do it now in **Settings > Wallet & Apple Pay > Apple Cash**, then tap **Verify Identity**. According to [Apple's support page](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109312), if you don't see that option, you've already verified. MacRumors adds that on iOS 27, people in states that support digital driver's licenses can use the license stored in Wallet to do it.

## If you don't verify

Your money doesn't get frozen. Anyone who can't be verified won't be able to send, receive, or add money, but whatever is already in the account can still be spent with Apple Pay or moved out to a bank account.

Apple's stated reason is "fraud protection and regulatory reasons." Its support page uses the same wording for both Apple Cash and Apple Card.

## The other two changes in the email

Apple's email flags two more updates to Green Dot's [terms and conditions](https://applecash.greendot.com/termsconditions/):

- **Bank and merchant funding, eventually.** Green Dot may enable what the terms call "Externally-Initiated Funding Transactions," meaning money could be pushed into Apple Cash directly from a bank or a merchant. Today the only way to reload is a linked debit or prepaid card in Wallet. There's no date for this.
- **Revised arbitration terms.** Green Dot has updated the arbitration and dispute resolution section, and Apple is encouraging users to read it. It's worth a look if you use Apple Cash regularly, since that section covers your rights if something goes wrong.

## Why it matters

Apple Cash has slowly turned into a real peer-to-peer payments product, with Tap to Cash, Apple Cash Family, and transfers in Messages. More money moving through it means more fraud pressure and more attention from regulators, and an ID check before any money goes in is the obvious fix. It's a bit more friction for casual users, but for most people it only happens once.

The more interesting line is the one about bank and merchant funding. Being able to load Apple Cash straight from a bank account, or have a merchant pay a refund or payout into it, would make it look a lot more like Venmo or Cash App. Apple and Green Dot are only saying it *may* happen. For now, the practical advice is easy: if you use Apple Cash, verify before November 9 so your next reload doesn't fail.

Apple Cash is U.S.-only and requires users to be 18 or older, though younger family members can use it through Apple Cash Family.

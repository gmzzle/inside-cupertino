Apple just brought back its World Travel Adapter Kit for $39 after pulling it last year, and if you're wondering why that's news, it's because they also changed the physical plug standard on all their Mac chargers. [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/28/apple-new-world-travel-adapter-kit/) spotted the new "AC Connector 2" rolling out across the 70W and 140W power adapters, extension cables, and the revived travel kit.

The change means your old extension cable won't fit new chargers, and your old travel adapters won't either. Apple says it's for "updated regulations," which is probably true—these things don't happen in a vacuum—but it doesn't make the transition less annoying. The original AC Connector had a notched design; the new one is pill-shaped. Same job, different shape, zero backward compatibility.

The World Travel Adapter Kit itself is actually useful if you move between regions regularly. You get five plugs covering North America, Europe, India, the UK, and a few other markets. It's not every plug standard on Earth, but it hits the big ones. At $39 it's not cheap for what's basically molded plastic, but it's less than buying individual adapters at airport kiosks, and it all fits in one box.

What's frustrating is that Apple introduced the new connector on MacBook Pro chargers earlier this year without updating the accessories to match. So if you bought a new MacBook Pro in the spring and tried to use your old travel kit, you were out of luck. Now they've fixed it, but only by making you buy new versions of things you might already own.

This is the part where someone will say it's about safety standards and regulatory compliance, and sure, that's real. But Apple's also been happy to let customers sit in compatibility limbo for months while they sorted out the accessory line. The new stuff works. The old stuff doesn't. Plan accordingly.

[AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/08/25/m6-mac-mini-arrives-in-ram-and-ssd-constrained-environment?utm_source=rss) reports the M6 Mac mini is here, and it's pricier than ever. The external design is unchanged from the M4 revision—still that palm-sized aluminum box—but the starting price hit a new high. That's a bold move for a product category Apple has historically positioned as the accessible Mac.

The August timing is genuinely strange. Apple doesn't do Mac refreshes in late summer. October and November are the usual slots for Mac updates, timed for the holiday push. An August drop suggests either inventory problems with the M4 model (two-month wait times before this announcement support that theory) or a shift in Apple's silicon cadence that nobody saw coming. Either way, it's off-pattern.

The M5 Pro option is the more interesting spec bump here. The base M6 is fine—expected performance gains, the usual efficiency story—but the Pro variant gives this tiny machine enough horsepower for actual pro workflows. Video editors and developers have been using Mac minis in server racks and home studios for years because the thermals are surprisingly good. More power in the same thermal envelope makes that use case even stronger, assuming Apple didn't cheap out on the cooling.

But that price increase stings. The Mac mini used to be the "oh, you already have a monitor and keyboard? Here's a Mac for less than a grand" option. Pushing the entry point higher narrows the gap with the iMac and makes the mini less of an obvious recommendation for switchers or people building budget home servers. Apple's betting that M6 performance justifies the premium, but the mini's whole appeal was value.

If you want one, order early—AppleInsider's right that these will sell out fast, especially if supply is already tight enough to cause two-month backlogs on the outgoing model.

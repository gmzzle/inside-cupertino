Meta just dropped Muse Spark Open, [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/15/meta-launches-new-muse-spark-open-model-optimized-to-run-on-mac-studio/) reported, and here's the detail that matters: it's specifically optimized for Mac Studio with M5 Ultra. Not "runs fine on Mac." Optimized for a specific Mac configuration. That's deliberate.

This is Meta doing what Apple won't—treating the Mac as serious AI hardware. Apple Intelligence still leans heavily on cloud processing for anything complex, even on M-series chips that could theoretically handle more locally. Meta's betting that developers and power users want to run capable models entirely on-device, and they're building for Apple's most powerful silicon to prove it. The Mac Studio becomes an AI workstation by default, not because Apple positioned it that way, but because Meta did.

The "open-weight" framing is the other play here. Apple Intelligence is closed, proprietary, and tightly controlled. Muse Spark Open is downloadable, modifiable, and runs without phoning home. For developers who've watched Apple lock down every aspect of on-device ML, this is the opposite approach—and it runs better on Mac hardware than Apple's own consumer-facing AI does. That's awkward.

Meta's also creating a wedge. If you're buying a Mac Studio for video editing or development, you now have a top-tier AI model that ships ready to run locally. Apple Intelligence isn't bad, but it's not positioned as the tool for people doing heavy lifting. Meta just filled that gap, on Apple's hardware, with an open model Apple would never release.

Apple built the silicon. Meta's building the software people actually want to run on it.

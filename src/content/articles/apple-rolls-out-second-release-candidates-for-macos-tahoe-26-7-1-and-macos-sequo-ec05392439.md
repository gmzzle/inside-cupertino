[9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/21/apple-rolls-out-second-release-candidates-for-macos-tahoe-26-7-1-and-macos-sequoia-15-8-1/) reported that Apple dropped second release candidates for macOS Tahoe 26.7.1 and Sequoia 15.8.1 today, less than a week after RC1. That's not the usual cadence.

Typically, an RC goes out, developers test for a few days, and if nothing breaks, that same build ships to the public with a new version number. When you see RC2 this quickly, it means someone found something—probably not catastrophic, but enough that Apple didn't want to ship RC1 as-is. Could be a kernel panic under specific conditions, a bug in a security fix, or something equally unglamorous. Point releases like these are supposed to be boring. Two RCs in five days means this one briefly wasn't.

What's actually in these updates? Apple hasn't said, which is standard for dot-releases this late in a cycle. Tahoe 26.7.1 likely includes security patches and bug fixes for the year-old OS that most enterprise Macs are still running. Sequoia 15.8.1 is probably similar—refinements, not features. Neither is the marquee release. That's macOS 27, which is presumably in beta and will ship in a few weeks alongside new hardware.

The dual-OS support window is Apple's new normal. Tahoe launched in late 2025, so it's still getting updates through at least fall 2027. Sequoia, one version older, has another year or so of patches ahead. For IT departments, this is actually useful—they can stay on the older OS while testing the new one without feeling abandoned. For everyone else, it's just noise. Most people installed Tahoe months ago and won't notice when 26.7.1 silently downloads.

Second RCs aren't a crisis, but they're a tell. Apple found something worth fixing before these builds hit a hundred million Macs. Expect the public release within a week, assuming RC2 holds up.

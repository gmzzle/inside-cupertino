Apple has a reputation for squeezing suppliers: short contracts, then a push for a lower price at every renewal. So the latest supply-chain report out of Korea is worth a closer look, because it describes Apple doing the opposite.

According to Korea's [Maeil Business Newspaper](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/business/12211344), Apple and Samsung Electronics are in final talks on a long-term agreement (LTA) for DRAM, the memory inside every iPhone, iPad, and Mac. The report was picked up by [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/17/apple-rare-long-term-memory-deal-samsung/), [iClarified](https://www.iclarified.com/103602/apple-nears-long-term-samsung-memory-deal-amid-supply-shortage-report), and [TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/11/18/news-samsung-apple-reportedly-finalizing-4-5-year-dram-lta-with-minimum-supply-commitment-starting-in-2027/). Neither company has confirmed it, and the deal is described as close, not signed.

## What the deal reportedly looks like

Based on Maeil's reporting:

- **Length:** four to five years
- **Start:** the first half of 2027
- **Volume:** a guaranteed minimum amount of DRAM from Samsung
- **Price:** not locked; it would be set later based on market conditions

That last point matters. Apple is not buying cheap memory for five years. It is buying *certainty that the memory shows up*, and agreeing to keep paying whatever the market says it's worth.

Maeil also reports that Apple recently signed a similar long-term deal with SK hynix, on terms favorable to the memory maker. According to the paper, Apple was the only big tech company without a Samsung LTA.

## Why Apple would change its playbook

Short answer: AI data centers are eating the memory supply.

Memory makers have been shifting production toward high-margin server memory for AI. That leaves less capacity for the mobile and PC chips Apple needs. TrendForce says mobile DRAM supply remains tight and forecasts conventional DRAM contract prices to rise 10–15% quarter over quarter in Q4 2026.

Apple has said as much itself. As [EconoTimes](https://econotimes.com/Report-Apple-Samsung-Electronics-Near-Multiyear-DRAM-Supply-Deal-1778300) notes, Apple's 2026 annual filing warns that supply-and-demand imbalances are constraining supplies and raising costs for memory and storage, and that it expects those pressures to get worse.

When the problem is *getting* parts at all, leverage on price matters less than a place in line. Samsung, SK hynix, and Micron have all been signing LTAs with major customers, and Apple waiting on the sidelines would mean competing for whatever is left.

## What's in it for Samsung

Predictability. TrendForce points out that Apple's orders are tied to phones and PCs, not AI infrastructure. If AI data center spending ever slows, a multi-year consumer-electronics commitment from the world's biggest phone maker is a useful hedge for Samsung and SK hynix.

## What it means for you

Probably not much this year, but it's a signal about the next few.

1. **Supply should be steadier.** The point of the deal is to make sure iPhones and Macs don't get stuck waiting on memory.
2. **Costs aren't going down.** With prices floating and reportedly high minimum pricing in recent LTAs, Maeil suggests memory costs could stay elevated even if the market cools.
3. **Watch the RAM upgrade prices.** Apple usually absorbs component swings in base models, but memory is exactly what it charges extra for on Macs and iPads. If costs stay high, that's the first place they're likely to show up.

None of that is confirmed, and Apple doesn't comment on supplier contracts. But a company famous for short leashes signing up for five years tells you how tight memory has gotten.

The driver who crashed through the Hingham, Massachusetts Apple Store back in 2022, killing one person and injuring over a dozen others, just had his bail revoked. [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/08/23/crime-blotter-bail-revoked-in-hingham-apple-store-crash-case?utm_source=rss) reported the development as part of their weekly crime roundup, but the timing here is worth noting: it took two years for the court to pull bail on a case that involved a car deliberately driven through a storefront full of people.

The Hingham crash was one of those stories that briefly made Apple Stores feel less safe than they'd ever felt before. Bradley Rein, the driver, was charged with second-degree murder. The victim, Kevin Bradley, was a 65-year-old construction manager working at the store. The initial investigation suggested the crash wasn't an accident, but the legal process has dragged on while Rein remained free on bail. That just changed, though the trial itself still hasn't happened.

Meanwhile, AirTags continue their dual life as both useful tracking devices and stalking tools. This week's blotter includes yet another arrest for AirTag stalking, a pattern that's become routine enough that these cases barely register as news anymore. Apple added anti-stalking features years ago—alerts when an unknown AirTag is traveling with you, sound notifications, cooperation with Android—but the product's core function is tracking, and bad actors keep using it exactly as designed, just with bad intent.

The other cases this week are standard Apple theft fare: iPhones stolen in San Francisco that turned up in Los Angeles, and devices seized from disgraced former Congressman George Santos, who apparently had an iPhone and MacBook among his possessions when authorities came calling. That last detail isn't surprising—everyone has an iPhone—but it's a reminder that Apple gear shows up in every kind of crime scene, from petty theft to federal investigations. When your products are everywhere, they're everywhere.

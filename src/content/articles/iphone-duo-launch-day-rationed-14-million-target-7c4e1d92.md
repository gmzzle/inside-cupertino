The iPhone Duo is officially on sale, and launch day looked a little different than iPhone launches used to. There were lines in Sydney, Shanghai, London, Chicago and on Fifth Avenue. But as [Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/articles/apple-bets-iphone-duo-remake-200340333.html) put it, with sales rationed there were fewer of the around-the-block queues and overnight camp-outs that once defined the day.

That isn't a sign of weak demand. It's what happens when Apple has far more buyers than phones and decides to hand out what it has slowly. And the more interesting news this week came from the supply chain, not the stores.

## What launch day actually looked like

- **Executives split up.** CEO John Ternus opened Apple's Domain Northside store in Austin with retail chief Deirdre O'Brien, high-fiving customers on the way in, according to [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/23/iphone-duo-launch-day-lines-form-at-apple-stores-walk-in-availability-limited/) and a CBS Austin clip cited by Reuters. The Formula 1 U.S. Grand Prix is in Austin this weekend, and Apple is F1's exclusive U.S. streamer. Marketing chief Greg Joswiak handled Fifth Avenue in New York.
- **Walk-in stock was thin.** Apple had already warned that stores would have "limited" units. 9to5Mac reports staff telling shoppers who missed out to check the Apple Store app Saturday at 10 a.m. Eastern for in-store availability.
- **It varied by store.** [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/23/iphone-duo-apple-store-pickup-available-today/) walked into Apple Edinburgh early and bought one without trouble, and staff there said they'd received more units than expected. Don't count on that everywhere.
- **Caps and resellers.** U.S. purchases are limited to two per customer. Reuters spoke with a buyer in Hong Kong who pre-ordered a 256GB Duo specifically to flip it for a profit of a few thousand Hong Kong dollars.

Apple's own [Newsroom post](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/iphone-duo-arrives-at-apple-store-locations-worldwide/) leaned on photos of crowds at Covent Garden, Mumbai and Shanghai, plus new Today at Apple sessions including one called "Say Hello to iPhone Duo."

## The bigger signal: 14 million

The rationing makes more sense next to a report from Taiwan's [DigiTimes](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20261022PD219/iphone-apple-shipments-demand-foldable.html) this week. It says Apple has raised its 12-month iPhone Duo shipment target by about 44%, from 9.7 million units to 14 million, and passed the new numbers to Foxconn and component suppliers.

[MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/22/apple-boosts-iphone-duo-orders/) notes the catch: hitting that number depends on assembly progress, after earlier reports of manufacturing trouble with the Duo's displays and hinges. A separate DigiTimes report [summarized by MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/23/iphone-duo-demand-exceeds-expectations/) says pre-orders beat Apple's expectations in several major markets, and that Duo demand could help offset softer iPhone 18 Pro sales.

That fits what buyers have seen. U.S. online shipping estimates slipped to January 25 within half an hour of pre-orders opening on October 16, and Apple called the response "nothing short of extraordinary." Telling suppliers to build more is one thing. Building a brand-new hinge and folding display at that pace is another.

## Ternus's first real test

This is the first major launch since Ternus took over as CEO in September, and Reuters frames the Duo as an early test of whether Apple can do to foldables what the iPhone did to smartphones. Samsung and Huawei have sold premium foldables for years.

The analysts are bullish. IDC expects Apple to take 44% of foldable market value in 2026 on 31% of units, and to lead the category in both value and units in 2027, per Reuters. IDC's Francisco Jeronimo said the next test is "turning excitement into availability, adoption" and a lasting market. DigiTimes forecasts global foldable shipments to grow 25.7% this year and 59.1% next year, with Apple's entry a big part of that.

Reviews are a different story. Reuters quotes John Gruber describing the Duo as "an extraordinarily polished prototype," and Marques Brownlee saying that for someone who mostly texts and browses, it's "probably a no." The missing telephoto lens and apps that still aren't built for the inner screen are the usual complaints.

## A fitting date

Apple picked an interesting day for this. October 23 is the 25th anniversary of the original iPod, which Steve Jobs unveiled at Infinite Loop in 2001 for $399. [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/) points out that early reaction to the iPod was mixed too, with readers calling it overpriced and "far from revolutionary." MacRumors also spotted a playable first-generation iPod hidden on Apple Music's web 404 page, surfaced on the anniversary.

## What this means

- **If you didn't get one today, Saturday is your next shot.** Check the Apple Store app Saturday morning for pickup reservations rather than just showing up at a store.
- **Online orders aren't moving soon.** Late January is still the honest expectation for a new order. Watch for those dates to move up if the higher build target actually holds.
- **Scarcity is part of the strategy, whether Apple means it or not.** A $1,999 phone that's hard to get feels more like a hit than one stacked on shelves. The real test is what the line looks like in March, when the phones are easy to find and the reviews have sunk in.

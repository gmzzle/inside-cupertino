iOS 27 dropped today with Siri AI, Apple's long-awaited overhaul of its perpetually behind assistant. [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/14/here-are-the-requirements-to-get-siri-ai-in-ios-27/) reported the feature comes with "several requirements," which in Apple-speak means a chunk of people who can install iOS 27 won't actually get the headline feature.

The requirements are predictable: you need an iPhone 15 Pro or newer. That's because Siri AI leans heavily on the Neural Engine in the A17 Pro and later chips, processing most requests on-device rather than bouncing everything to the cloud. Apple's pitched this as a privacy win, and to their credit, it is. But it also means anyone with an iPhone 15 base model or older is stuck with the same Siri that still can't reliably set two timers.

This isn't new territory. Apple pulled the same move with Apple Intelligence in iOS 18, gating features behind the A17 Pro. The difference is Siri AI actually feels like the thing Apple Intelligence was supposed to be two years ago—conversational follow-ups that work, cross-app actions that don't require you to repeat context, and finally, *finally*, a "who's this?" that works when you're looking at a photo. It's the kind of upgrade that makes the device requirement sting more, because for once Siri might be worth using.

The RAM matters too. Siri AI needs at least 8GB to run its language models locally without turning your phone into a hand warmer. The iPhone 15 base model shipped with 6GB, which is why it's left out despite having a Neural Engine. Apple could've made a cloud-fallback version for older devices, but that would've undermined the entire privacy angle they've been shouting about since WWDC 2025.

So if you've got an iPhone 14 Pro or an iPhone 15, congrats: you can install iOS 27, but Siri's still the same assistant that tells you it can't help with that. The cutoff is annoying, but at least this time it's tied to actual hardware constraints and not just Apple being stingy with features.

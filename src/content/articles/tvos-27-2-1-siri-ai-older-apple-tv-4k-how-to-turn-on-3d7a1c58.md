On the same day the new $299 Apple TV 4K went on sale, Apple gave a lot of existing owners a reason to keep the box they already have. [tvOS 27.2.1](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/13/apple-releases-tvos-27-2-1/), released Friday afternoon, brings Siri AI to the second- and third-generation Apple TV 4K.

The catch is that installing the update doesn't turn anything on. Here's how to get it working, and why your HomePod mini 2 didn't get the same treatment.

## Which Apple TVs get it

tvOS 27.2.1 is available for every Apple TV 4K that runs tvOS 27, but the Siri AI part only applies to the [second- and third-generation models](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/13/apple-releases-tvos-27-2-1-and-homepod-27-2-1-heres-whats-new/). The new fourth-generation box already has Siri AI on its own tvOS 27.2 build. The original Apple TV 4K and the Apple TV HD don't run tvOS 27, so they're out, according to [MacRumors' device breakdown](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/14/apple-home-devices-siri-ai/).

The update also includes security fixes, so it's worth installing even if you don't care about the new Siri.

## How to turn it on

1. On your Apple TV, open **Settings > System > Software Update** and install tvOS 27.2.1.
2. Once it restarts, go to **Settings > General > Siri** and turn on **Siri AI (Beta)**. That starts a download for the feature.

If you skip step two, nothing changes. [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/13/homepod-mini-2-still-lacks-siri-ai/) notes that the older Siri, which Apple now calls Siri Classic, stays on by default after the update.

## What you need for it to work

Siri AI on the Apple TV leans on your iPhone. The owner of the home in the Home app needs an Apple Intelligence-capable iPhone or iPad, which means an iPhone 15 Pro or later, running current software with Siri AI turned on and set to a supported language. Other people in the house don't need one for general requests, but personal requests like "what's on my calendar" need a paired Apple Intelligence device.

Language and location matter too. On Apple's home devices, Siri AI is English only and limited to the U.S., Canada, the UK, Australia, New Zealand, India, Singapore and South Africa. It isn't available in the EU. Apple still labels it a beta, which explains the "(Beta)" in the settings toggle.

## HomePod mini 2: still waiting

Apple also shipped HomePod Software 27.2.1 on Friday, and some HomePod mini 2 owners hoped it would be the update that switches on Siri AI. It isn't. Apple's release notes say only that it "provides bug fixes," and [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/13/homepod-mini-2-still-lacks-siri-ai/) confirmed Siri AI is not part of it. Apple's site still says the feature is "coming in a future software update," with no date.

When it does arrive, expect the HomePod mini 2 to rely heavily on your iPhone as well. MacRumors reports the speaker has 2GB of RAM, well below what on-device Siri AI needs, so personal requests will run through a paired iPhone or iPad. If you bought one for Siri AI, you're buying on Apple's promise for now.

## Our take

This morning we [suggested](/articles/homeview-apple-tv-4k-launch-day-what-to-know-b71e4a90/) that if your current Apple TV 4K works, the new one is a hard sell. Today's update makes that easier to say. A second- or third-generation owner with an iPhone 15 Pro or newer now gets Siri AI for free. What the $299 model still adds is the A19 chip, microphones that let you talk to Siri without picking up the remote, Wi-Fi 7, and four-HomePod surround sound.

Install the update this weekend, flip the toggle, and try it for a few days before deciding whether hands-free Siri is worth $299 to you.

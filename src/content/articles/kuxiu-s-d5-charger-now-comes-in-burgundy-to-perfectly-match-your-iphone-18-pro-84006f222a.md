Kuxiu is doing something clever that Apple refuses to: making accessories that actually match the iPhone's finish. [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/11/kuxius-d5-charger-now-comes-in-burgundy-to-perfectly-match-your-iphone-18-pro/) reports the company just released a burgundy D5 5-in-1 charging station to pair with the iPhone 18 Pro's new hero color, following last year's Cosmic Orange versions of their X40 and S4 chargers.

This matters because Apple spent years pushing its iPhone colors as lifestyle choices, then abandoned the accessory side of that pitch. You can buy a Desert Titanium iPhone 16 Pro, but good luck finding a first-party charging puck or battery pack in anything but white or black. Kuxiu saw the gap and sprinted through it. Their color matching is apparently good enough that people mistake these chargers for official Apple products, which is either impressive manufacturing or a sign Apple's gotten too predictable with its Pantone picks.

The D5 itself is a 5-in-1 station with built-in cooling, which suggests Kuxiu is targeting the power-user crowd that's stacking an iPhone, Apple Watch, AirPods, and maybe an iPad or Vision Pro on one desk. Pricing wasn't disclosed in the source piece, but their previous chargers ran $80-120, undercutting Belkin and Anker's premium lines while offering something neither does: vanity.

Apple killed its AirPower mat and hasn't shipped a multi-device charger since. Meanwhile, third-party brands are building entire product lines around Apple's yearly color drops. That's revenue Apple used to capture with cases and docks, now going to companies fast enough to match a paint chip. Kuxiu's betting that people who spend $1,199 on a burgundy phone want their desk setup to match, and based on their second year doing this, the bet's paying off.

If Apple's serious about ecosystem lock-in, letting accessory makers own the aesthetic lane seems like an odd choice.

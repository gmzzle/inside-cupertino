Apple is spending this week putting indie games on the App Store's front page, and the biggest name in the mix is one you already know from consoles and PCs.

In a [Newsroom post](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/app-store-indie-games-week-kicks-off-today/) published today, Apple said Indie Games Week is live, with seven acclaimed indie titles available to download or pre-order on iPhone and iPad. The App Store will spotlight them with daily features and curated collections all week. [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/12/app-store-indie-games-week/) flagged the announcement this morning.

## The seven debuts

Apple's list mixes brawlers, deck builders, and open-world wanderers:

- **Sifu**, from Sloclap and Kepler Interactive — the kung fu revenge brawler, adapted for iPhone and iPad with custom touch controls, a reworked camera, and full controller support.
- **Vampire Crawlers**, from poncle and Nosebleed Interactive — a gothic card battler spun out of *Vampire Survivors*.
- **No Man's Sky**, from Hello Games — mobile debut on October 28.
- **Little Kitty, Big City**, from Double Dagger Studio.
- **9 Kings**, from Sad Socket and Playdigious.
- **Dome Keeper**, from Bippinbits and Raw Fury.
- **Yes, Your Grace 2: Snowfall**, from Brave At Night and Noodlecake Studios.

The last four are available to download now, alongside Sifu and Vampire Crawlers. No Man's Sky is the holdout with a firm ship date later this month.

## No Man's Sky, in your pocket

Hello Games' space sandbox turns ten this year, and Apple says the iPhone and iPad version ships with every major expansion to date, including the recent Cosmos update that lets players run their own space stations. Apple frames it as a full runtime universe on a phone: explore, trade, fight, and survive across a procedurally generated galaxy.

That is a different kind of App Store bet than another endless runner. If the port holds up, it is one of the clearest proof points yet that Apple wants bigger, longer-session games on iPhone, not just quick hits between meetings.

## Why Apple is doing this now

Carson Oliver, Apple's head of App Store Worldwide, cast the week as a celebration of "games and the teams that take creative risks," from solo developers to growing studios. The timing is not accidental. Apple has spent the past few years pushing Arcade, controller support, and higher-end silicon as reasons to treat the iPhone as a gaming device. A themed week of known indie hits is the marketing version of that argument.

It also sits next to a crowded October. The [iPhone Duo](/articles/iphone-duo-demo-units-limited-supply-ea942f2e/) is days from preorders, and Tuesday's [Welcome home](/articles/apple-homeview-mid-november-apple-tv-homepod-mini-oct-30-707c2707/) event is about to flood the timeline with smart-home hardware. Indie Games Week gives the App Store something to talk about that is not a new chassis or a new chip.

## What to do this week

If you already play on a controller with your iPhone or iPad, Sifu is the obvious first stop. If you want the bigger experiment, set a reminder for No Man's Sky on October 28. And if you are just browsing, Apple says the daily features and curated collections will keep rotating the seven titles through the storefront all week — which is as close as the App Store gets to a festival lineup.

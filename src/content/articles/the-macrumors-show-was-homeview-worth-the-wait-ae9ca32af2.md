Apple spent half a decade letting Amazon and Google own the smart display category, then showed up with a $299 square screen that does exactly what you'd expect and nothing more. [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/16/the-macrumors-show-homeview-worth-the-wait/) covered the announcement and the lukewarm response, which makes sense once you think about who this thing is actually for.

The core problem: if you already have an iPhone, iPad, and Apple Watch, HomeView is redundant. Those guided recipe cards? Your iPad does that, propped against the backsplash. Smart home controls? Your phone is already in your pocket. The 7-inch display dims to 0.3 nits and shows customizable faces, which sounds impressive until you realize your Watch does the same thing on your wrist. Apple's own promotional materials can't quite explain why you need this if you're already deep in the ecosystem.

For Android switchers or people replacing an Echo Show, it's a cleaner pitch. No ads on the lock screen, actual HomeKit integration that works, FaceTime that doesn't look like garbage. But that's a narrow audience, especially at $299. The Echo Show 10 is $249 and has a motorized display that follows you around the kitchen. The Nest Hub Max is $229. Apple's betting people will pay a premium for build quality and privacy, which historically works - but only when the product itself feels essential.

The timing is what really stings. This should have shipped in 2021 when everyone was stuck at home buying smart displays and video doorbells. Instead, it arrives after the pandemic bump faded and the competition became deeply entrenched. HomePod mini still hasn't cracked double-digit market share. The Apple TV 4K is great but niche. Launching a third piece of living room hardware that overlaps with both feels like Apple doing homework nobody assigned anymore.

HomeView will sell fine to the faithful, but it's not going to expand Apple's smart home footprint in any meaningful way. It's a nice-to-have for people who were already buying everything Apple makes, which means it's also completely skippable.

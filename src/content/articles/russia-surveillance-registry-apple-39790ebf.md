Russia has added Apple to the government list that obliges tech companies to keep users' messages and turn them over to security services when asked. Roskomnadzor, the country's communications regulator, put Apple and Microsoft on its registry of "organizers of information dissemination" on **October 2**, [Bloomberg first reported](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/28/russia-now-requires-apple-to-store-user-communications-and-hand-them-over-if-requested/) this week after spotting the entries in the regulator's database. Apple's listing number is 594-PP, according to [VZGLYAD, citing TASS](https://vz.ru/news/2026/10/28/1468973.html).

## What the registry requires

Companies on the list must, under Russian law:

- **Store Russian users' correspondence**, including audio and video messages, and hand that data to security services on request.
- **Keep records about users and how their messages were sent** inside Russia for three years, per Bloomberg as quoted by [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/28/russia-now-requires-apple-to-store-user-communications-and-hand-them-over-if-requested/).

Firms that don't comply face fines and, eventually, restrictions on access to their services in Russia, [Quartz reports](https://qz.com/russia-apple-microsoft-surveillance-registry-roskomnadzor-102826). Apple joins WhatsApp, Telegram, Snapchat, Cloudflare and Blizzard on the list, and Google and Google Meet were added in January.

It's not clear whether Apple was added at its own request or by force. Russian outlet [NEWS.ru](https://news.ru/vlast/apple-i-microsoft-popali-v-reestr-roskomnadzora) notes that the regulator's database doesn't say. Quartz points out that the regulator can list a company when federal security or law-enforcement agencies ask, and since 2024 it can list one against its will after a court rules against it. Access to Apple's websites in Russia isn't being restricted for now.

## Apple hasn't said what it will do

As of Wednesday, neither Apple nor Microsoft had commented or said whether it plans to comply, [9to5Mac reports](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/28/russia-now-requires-apple-to-store-user-communications-and-hand-them-over-if-requested/). That silence is the real story. Apple builds iMessage and FaceTime with end-to-end encryption, which it says means [even Apple can't read the content](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102651). A rule that says "store the messages and hand them over" collides directly with a system designed so the company doesn't hold readable messages in the first place.

There are a few ways this could go. Apple could hand over the metadata it does hold and nothing more, the regulator could fine it, or Russia could restrict more of its services. Watch for a fine or a new block first.

## How we got here

This is the latest step in a long squeeze:

- Apple **stopped selling products in Russia** in March 2022 after the invasion of Ukraine, and limited Apple Pay there, [MacRumors notes](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/30/apple-russian-state-surveillance-registry/).
- Russia **blocked FaceTime** in December 2025, claiming it was being used for terrorism, recruitment and fraud. It had already limited WhatsApp and Telegram calls and blocked Signal and Viber.
- In August, Russia's antitrust regulator opened a case against Apple for not preinstalling the state-backed MAX messenger and the RuStore app store, per [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/28/russia-now-requires-apple-to-store-user-communications-and-hand-them-over-if-requested/).

## Does this affect you?

If you don't live in Russia, not directly. The rules cover Russian users and data stored in Russia. It matters for everyone else because it's another test of Apple's line on privacy. Apple has refused to build backdoors into its encryption elsewhere, and how it answers Moscow will show how firmly it holds that line when the price is access to a market.

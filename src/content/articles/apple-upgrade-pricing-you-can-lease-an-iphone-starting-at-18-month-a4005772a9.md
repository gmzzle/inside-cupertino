[MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/07/28/apple-upgrade-program-pricing/) has the pricing breakdown for Apple Upgrade, and it's about what you'd expect: monthly payments that look manageable until you multiply them out. An iPhone 17e runs $17.99/month for 24 months. That's $432 over two years for a phone that costs, what, $599 retail? You're paying 72% of the device cost to rent it, then either handing it back or paying extra to own it.

The real story here is Klarna handling the backend. Apple's not technically financing this themselves—they're white-labeling a lease through a buy-now-pay-later company that's spent the last few years trying to look like a bank. That means Klarna's credit checks, Klarna's terms, and Klarna's leverage if you want to actually keep the device at the end. Apple gets to offer flexible payments without taking on the lending risk or regulatory headache. Smart for them. Less clear what's in it for you unless you're the kind of person who needs a new iPhone every September no matter what.

Compare this to the iPhone Upgrade Program Apple's had since 2015. That one bundles AppleCare+, lets you trade up after 12 months, and you're financing-to-own through Citizens Bank. Apple Upgrade is a lease with no AppleCare included and no early upgrade path—just 24 months of payments, then a decision. It's not an upgrade program. It's a rental program with an option to buy at the end, which is functionally what every car lease has been for decades.

The pricing isn't outrageous, but it's not a deal either. If you're confident you'll want the iPhone 19 in two years, leasing the 17 makes some sense. If there's any chance you'll want to keep it, or sell it yourself, or skip a generation, you're better off buying outright or using Apple's actual upgrade financing. This is Apple expanding optionality for people who think in monthly payments, not Apple trying to save you money.

Klarna's involved because Apple wants the customer relationship without the credit exposure, and that tells you everything about who this program is really for.

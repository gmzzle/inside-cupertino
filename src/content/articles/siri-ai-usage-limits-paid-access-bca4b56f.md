Yesterday's earnings headlines were about memory chips and margins. The line that will matter more to most iPhone owners came out of the analyst Q&A: Siri AI isn't going to be all-you-can-eat forever.

Asked about iCloud+ and Apple Intelligence, CEO John Ternus said "there's going to be some usage limits on features that rely on server-side models, which include Siri AI and intelligent photo editing," and that "anyone who needs greater capacity will be able to purchase expanded access in the future," according to the [call transcript](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/aapl/transcripts/702819-q4-2026/). Apple didn't give a price, a launch date, or how big the free allowance will be.

## What Apple has actually put in writing

This isn't just a stray comment on a call. Apple has a [support page on Apple Intelligence usage limits](https://support.apple.com/en-us/127901) that spells out which features are covered. The list includes Siri AI, intelligent photo editing tools such as Clean Up, Extend and Spatial Reframing, Image Playground, the cloud Apple Foundation Models in Shortcuts, and third-party apps that use Apple's models in Private Cloud Compute.

According to that page, when you hit a limit, the feature becomes available again after a time-out period. Limits may vary by feature, request complexity and system demand, and "increased access to such features will be available for a fee."

The key distinction is where the work happens. Anything that runs on the iPhone itself costs Apple essentially nothing. "Obviously, on-device is very, very efficient, no real cost associated with that," CFO Kevan Parekh said on the call. "But on the server side, we would have some inference costs." Those cloud costs are what the limits are meant to manage.

## Why it's showing up now

Parekh tied Siri AI directly to the margin guide that sent the stock down. Apple's December-quarter gross margin forecast of 45% to 46% includes "some costs related to the ramp of Siri AI," on top of higher memory prices, [CNBC reports](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/03/apple-earnings-john-ternus-faces-first-test-amid-high-memory-costs.html). Apple will book those cloud costs in Services cost of sales, and it will defer a slice of hardware revenue to account for Siri AI as a bundled service, the same way it already handles Maps and iCloud.

In other words, every heavy Siri AI user is now a small cost line on the Services side of the business, at the same time Services growth is slowing to a guided 8% to 9%. Charging the heaviest users is the obvious way to keep that from getting worse.

## Where the paid tier probably lands

Ternus pointed to iCloud+ himself. "In the beginning, iCloud tiers were largely about storage, but we've been adding more capabilities and features to it," he said, noting plans that now include Apple TV and Apple Arcade in more than 100 countries and regions. [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/earnings/2026/apple-forecasts-slower-services-growth-as-ai-costs-rise/) and [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1025245/apple-earnings-q4-ai) both read it the same way.

There's already a precedent on that support page: some Apple Intelligence features in the Home app, like camera video summaries, are capped by iCloud+ tier, at one camera on the 2 TB plan, two on 6 TB and five on 12 TB. Expanded Siri AI access slotting into those same tiers would be the least surprising outcome. That's our read, not something Apple has announced.

## Our take

This is the right call made in the least satisfying way. Cloud AI costs real money every time someone uses it, and every other big AI company caps or charges for heavy use. Apple saying so now, while Siri AI is still labeled a beta, is better than springing a subscription on people in a year.

But Apple has spent the last two years selling new iPhones partly on the promise of Apple Intelligence, and its executives said again yesterday that AI offers "a compelling reason" for customers to upgrade. If the part of Siri that makes it worth upgrading for turns out to be metered, Apple needs to be very clear about how much the free allowance covers before it starts counting. Most people will never come close to a limit for setting timers and sending texts. The people who will are exactly the ones Apple was trying to win back from ChatGPT and Gemini.

The things to watch are the size of the free allowance, whether the paid access is a standalone add-on or tied to iCloud+ tiers, and whether there's any warning in Settings before you hit a time-out.

[9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/28/most-people-overpay-phone-plan-tello-mobile-maxes-out-at-25-month/) pointed out something obvious but worth repeating: most people are getting fleeced on their wireless bill. The average American pays somewhere between $60 and $100 a month for phone service, often without realizing there are plans a quarter of that price running on the exact same towers.

Tello Mobile is one of those MVNOs—mobile virtual network operators—that lease capacity from the big carriers instead of owning infrastructure. In Tello's case, it's T-Mobile's network, which means you get the same 5G coverage T-Mobile customers do, just without the T-Mobile bill. Their unlimited plan caps at $25/month. Entry-level plans start at $5. No contracts, no gotchas about "taxes and fees extra." It's legitimately cheap.

The tradeoff is deprioritization. During network congestion, MVNOs get pushed to the back of the line behind postpaid customers on the host carrier. In practice, that means slower data speeds if you're at a stadium or a busy airport. For most day-to-day use—texting, maps, streaming music on your commute—it's invisible. If you live in a rural area where T-Mobile is already struggling, though, this matters more.

The real question is why so few people switch. Inertia, mostly. Carrier stores make switching feel complicated. Family plans create perceived lock-in. And people assume cheap means bad, even when the network infrastructure is identical. Apple's push toward eSIM has quietly made switching easier—no need to wait for a physical SIM in the mail—but most iPhone users still don't know Tello exists.

If you're on an old grandfathered plan or genuinely need priority data for work, fine. But if you're paying $80/month out of habit, you're funding someone else's Super Bowl ad.

For three days, iPhone Duo owners have been posting the same photo: a thin line running down the fold of the inner screen, and a bright line shooting across it. We [covered the first reports on launch day](/articles/iphone-duo-hinge-screen-cracks-launch-day-9d3b6e41/), and yesterday [JerryRigEverything showed how sand in the hinge](/articles/iphone-duo-fails-jerryrigeverything-durability-test-4c8e2a71/) can take the phone out.

Today iFixit published its [full teardown](https://www.ifixit.com/News/119895/iphone-duo-teardown), and it's the closest thing yet to an explanation. It isn't a final answer. But it narrows down *where* the damage starts, and it gives owners a clearer picture of what a repair would involve.

## Where the cracks cluster

iFixit built a 3D model of the hinge from teardown footage, Apple's patents and CT scans, then lined it up against photos of damaged screens. In iFixit's words:

> "The breaks cluster near the transition from the gap for the crossing flex cable to the central hinge support. The damage we're seeing in photos isn't right where the display cable passes through the hinge, but slightly lower, at a point where several edges meet and change how the thin display is supported as it bends."

So the accordion-pleated cable that links the two halves, the usual suspect in any foldable, probably isn't the direct cause. The trouble spot is just below it, where several hinge parts meet under the screen.

iFixit floats a few possible culprits:

- **A support bar that doesn't drop far enough.** In iFixit's closing simulation, the center support bar doesn't retract fully, and the screen's curved fold comes down on it. That creates a small stress hotspot near where the breaks are being reported.
- **The curved hinge rails.** Per [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/25/ifixit-iphone-duo-teardown/) and [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/25/ifixit-tears-down-iphone-duo-finds-possible-causes-of-early-screen-failures/), iFixit also suggested the curved rails that guide the hinge may be rising above the flat plates as the phone closes. One of those rails lines up with the damage.
- **Something else entirely.** MacRumors reports iFixit listed pressure, a trapped particle, repeated bending or a manufacturing defect as possibilities. It also noted the screen may not have physically snapped at all, since a wiring fault can look the same.

That last point matches what iFixit wrote in its blog post. A tiny fault in the display's electrical connections can knock out a whole row or column of pixels, which explains why some owners see a big bright line but can't spot any other damage. Bottom line: lots of possible culprits, and no clear answer yet. Apple hasn't publicly explained the failures.

## The dust test: better than rivals, still crunchy

Before opening the phone, iFixit worked it open and closed in a bag of fluorescent powder. The Duo has an IP68 rating, and most of the dust stopped at the adhesive barriers around the electronics. Some still ended up in the USB-C port, speaker grilles and trim. And some reached the hinge's moving parts, which started to crunch.

The Duo collected less visible powder than the Galaxy Z Fold8 and Pixel 11 Pro Fold did in the same test. iFixit's verdict: "We hoped Apple would solve this problem, but sadly it seems like the Duo's got the same sand-phobia as its folding cousins."

iFixit also points out that its test isn't an IP test. IP ratings cover the device's enclosure, and the hinge mechanism doesn't seem to be included. Cleaning or replacing those moving parts means taking off the flexible display and its supports.

## What's good inside

The teardown isn't all bad news. iFixit calls the Duo the most repairable foldable it has taken apart, and gives several reasons:

- **Batteries that pop out with electricity.** Both cells use the electrically released adhesive Apple has been using on recent iPhones. iFixit called it "the best battery-removal experience we've seen in a foldable so far."
- **A robust outer display.** It came off intact, plastic frame included. That matters because many internal repairs go through it.
- **Trim that survives.** The inner screen's trim came off in one piece. iFixit notes the Galaxy Z Fold8's crumbled.
- **A flat layout.** The logic board is unstacked: a single layer, with some functions moved to a separate I/O board. iFixit notes almost every iPhone since the iPhone 8 has used a double-layer board. Cameras and charging hardware are easy to reach.
- **A replaceable cover layer.** The matte nano-texture layer on the inner screen peels off and can be replaced without swapping the OLED underneath. iFixit stresses it's not a DIY screen protector. Apple's procedure uses special fixtures and precise alignment.

iFixit also peeled back some of the inner display's ultrathin layers (Apple describes it as a 10-layer stack) and put them under a microscope. It found the nano-texture is made of tiny rounded bumps that scatter reflections to hide the crease, plus oddly oriented oval subpixels that may help with that too.

## A provisional 5 out of 10

iFixit gave the iPhone Duo a provisional repairability score of **5 out of 10**. That's its highest ever for a foldable. The Galaxy Z Fold8 and Pixel 11 Pro Fold both got 4. The score is provisional because iFixit is still waiting on replacement parts, which it expects through Apple's Self Service Repair program.

The big deduction is the inner display and hinge, which take a lot of work and money to replace. As MacRumors quoted iFixit: "This is the most repairable foldable smartphone we've seen so far, and it's still going to be a bear to repair."

## What this means for owners

- **Keep it out of sand and grit.** Two separate tests in two days have shown debris getting into the hinge. iFixit's closing advice: "You'd better baby that screen, Duo owners. And maybe quit carrying around that pocket sand." Beach days, sandboxes and the bottom of a gym bag are the risky places.
- **Don't force a tight hinge.** Owners describing the failure often mention a noise while opening or closing the phone. If the hinge feels gritty or stiff, stop and get it looked at rather than pushing through.
- **If your screen cracks, contact Apple now.** iFixit says Apple is offering replacement units for now. Out of warranty, inner-display damage costs $999.
- **Know the cover-layer price.** If the nano-texture layer gets scratched or worn, Apple will replace it for **$19 with AppleCare+** or **$49 without**, per [Apple's pricing as compiled by iFixit](https://www.ifixit.com/News/119797/how-the-iphone-duos-crazy-repair-prices-compare). Don't peel it off yourself.
- **Think hard about AppleCare+.** With AppleCare+, inner-display damage drops to a $99 fee, compared with $999 without it. Given the early failure reports, that gap is the strongest argument for coverage on this phone.
- **The return window is still open.** As we noted yesterday, MacRumors says buyers have two weeks to return the Duo. Anyone who bought on launch day and is having second thoughts still has time.

iFixit's model is a lead, not a diagnosis. The real answer will come from Apple, or from enough failed units to show a pattern. Until then, the teardown confirms what the first week has suggested: the Duo is well built in most places, and its weak spot is right in the middle.

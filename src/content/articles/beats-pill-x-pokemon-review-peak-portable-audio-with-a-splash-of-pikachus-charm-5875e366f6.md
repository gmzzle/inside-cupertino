[AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/10/16/beats-pill-x-pokemon-review-peak-portable-audio-with-a-splash-of-pikachus-charm?utm_source=rss) reviewed the Pokémon edition Beats Pill, and their reviewer's honesty is refreshing: they already own the regular black one but bought this anyway. That's the entire game here.

Apple relaunched the Beats Pill in June 2024 for $149.99 after discontinuing the previous model in 2022. The new version is legitimately good - improved bass, USB-C, 24-hour battery, IP67 water resistance. It's the portable speaker Beats should have made years ago. This Pokémon variant changes exactly none of that. Same guts, same price, different exterior featuring Pikachu and Mimikyu graphics. The reviewer calls it "a cute addition" with "visual flair," which is code for: this is a sticker job.

But here's the thing about Pokémon collaborations - they work because The Pokémon Company is weirdly disciplined about them. They don't slap Charizard on everything. When they do a tech partnership, it tends to be with brands that don't actively suck. Beats fits that now. The Pill is a solid speaker, so this isn't asking fans to buy garbage for the privilege of Pikachu ownership. It's asking them to pay the same price for a good speaker that also happens to scratch the collecting itch.

The real issue is availability. Limited collabs like this vanish fast, either into actual buyers' hands or straight to resale markup hell. If you're reading this review weeks after launch, you probably can't find one at retail anymore. That's by design. Scarcity makes the collaboration feel special even when the product inside is identical.

Apple has mostly avoided this playbook with its own hardware - no Mario AirPods, no Zelda Apple Watch bands from Cupertino itself. Beats operates with more freedom under the Apple umbrella, which is probably smart. Let the Beats brand chase the collab crowd while Apple proper stays minimal. Different audiences, same parent company revenue.

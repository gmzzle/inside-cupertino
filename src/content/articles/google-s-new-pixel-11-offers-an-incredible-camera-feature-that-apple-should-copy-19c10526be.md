[9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/22/pixel-11-new-camera-feature-apple-should-copy/) is making noise about the Pixel 11's new camera controls, and honestly, they're onto something. Google's letting you tweak processing settings before you shoot—think HDR intensity, noise reduction levels, that sort of thing. It's granular control over the computational stuff that usually just happens automatically.

Apple's approach has always been the opposite. The iPhone decides how much Deep Fusion to apply, how aggressive Smart HDR should be, whether Night mode fires. You get the final image Apple thinks looks best. For most people, most of the time, that works. The iPhone takes great photos. But when it doesn't work—when faces look plasticky or the sky is nuclear orange—you're stuck with it. You can't tell the phone to back off.

This isn't just a Pro feature thing, either. Apple has ProRAW, sure, but that's a different workflow entirely. You're not adjusting computational photography; you're bypassing it and editing later. What Google's doing is letting you shape the computational output at capture time. Want less aggressive HDR? Dial it down. Prefer grainier night shots with more detail? You can have that. It's the middle ground Apple has never offered.

The iPhone 18 Pro is probably locked at this point, so if Apple's going to steal this idea, it won't be until the iPhone 19. But they should. Computational photography has gotten so good that it's started to impose a house style on everything—the iPhone look, the Pixel look. Giving users some say in that process wouldn't undermine Apple's "it just works" philosophy. It would acknowledge that sometimes people know what they want their photo to look like better than an algorithm does.

Google figured that out first, which is rare enough to be annoying.

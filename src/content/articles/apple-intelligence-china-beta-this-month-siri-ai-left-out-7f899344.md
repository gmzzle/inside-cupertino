Yesterday the paper trail said Apple Intelligence was cleared for China's iPhones. Today Apple said it out loud — and put a month on it.

In an exclusive interview in Shanghai on Thursday, Apple executive chairman Tim Cook told [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34256510) (澎湃新闻) that Apple Intelligence has been approved for the Chinese market and that a test version is coming later in October. [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202610/15/WS6ad09bd8e4b06d4aa0563014.html) carried the same remarks in English. The first wave, per Cook, covers live translation, writing tools, and the Clean Up tool in Photos. Siri AI is not part of it.

That is the piece our [Wednesday look at the iPhone Duo's MIIT filings](/articles/china-clears-alibabas-qwen-for-iphone-duo-preorders-start-thursday-50e195ef/) was missing. Permits told us the models were registered. They did not tell us when anyone in China would actually get to tap a Writing Tools button. Now there is a rough calendar.

## Two dates, one rollout

Cook's "later this month" and the December date reported elsewhere fit together rather than conflict.

- **Late October: a test build.** That is Cook's own framing, per The Paper. It reads as a beta, not a general release.
- **December: the public launch.** [Bloomberg reported Wednesday](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/14/apple-intelligence-reportedly-coming-to-china-in-december-but-without-siri-ai/), as summarized by 9to5Mac, that Apple plans to ship Apple Intelligence in China as part of iOS 27.3 in December.

Apple has not published an iOS 27.3 date or a China feature page for this, so treat December as reporting, not an announcement. The beta timing is the part that now comes straight from Apple's chairman.

## What China gets first — and what it doesn't

The two accounts overlap but are not identical. Cook named live translation, writing tools, and Photos Clean Up. Bloomberg's list for the initial rollout, per 9to5Mac, is Genmoji, notification summaries, writing tools, and Clean Up. Both agree on writing tools and Clean Up as the foundation.

Both also agree on what is missing. Siri AI — the rebuilt assistant that headlines this year's software and the iPhone Duo launch — is out of the first wave. Bloomberg adds that Apple's in-house image-generation tools are excluded and that some text-generation features will be limited in certain apps.

In other words, the China launch is largely the original 2024-era Apple Intelligence set, arriving roughly two years after it debuted in the U.S. Bloomberg itself raises the obvious question: with Huawei, Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo, and others already shipping their own AI features, it is unclear how much a basic, late package moves the needle for Apple.

## Who does what: Alibaba and Baidu

The China version is not a straight port. According to Bloomberg, Apple built its China guardrails with Alibaba, including a filter that lets the government approve ongoing updates to the AI models, and is working with Baidu on search- and Siri-related features.

A separate [China Daily report](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202610/15/WS6ad0bab9e4b06d4aa0563074.html) citing people familiar with the matter fills in the split:

- **Alibaba** supplies the model capabilities behind text and image understanding and generation, with integration planned across iPhone, iPad, Mac, and Vision Pro software in China.
- **Baidu** handles connections to search, Siri-related functions, and Visual Intelligence.

China Daily notes that Baidu's Siri-related work is separate from the redesigned Siri AI assistant that Cook left out of the test release. So "Baidu powers Siri in China" would be overstating it — at least for now.

## Which iPhones qualify

The Paper points to Apple's China website, which lists Apple Intelligence support for the iPhone 15 Pro and 15 Pro Max, every iPhone 16 model and later, and iPhone Air. The September 29 permit updates that added the Apple Intelligence, Qwen, and Wanxiang registrations covered the iPhone Duo as well as iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone 17 Pro, and iPhone Air models, according to The Paper.

## The timing problem for the Duo

Here is the awkward bit. China Duo preorders open Friday, October 16, with phones shipping October 23. Siri AI is central to Apple's pitch this fall, from the new Apple TV 4K to HomeView — and it is not on the China calendar at all. Even the basic Apple Intelligence set reaches Chinese Duo owners as a beta first, with the broad release reported for December.

That leaves China in a similar spot to Europe, where Apple said in its HomeView footnotes this week that it is still working with the EU on a path to launch Siri AI. (We covered that [EU waiting game](/articles/siri-ai-eu-wait-best-case-spring-023b4980/) earlier.) Two of Apple's largest markets are getting the new hardware on schedule and the marquee assistant on someone else's.

## What to watch

- **The beta itself.** Whether it lands as a developer build, a public beta, or both — and whether Genmoji and notification summaries show up alongside the three features Cook named.
- **iOS 27.3.** Apple has not dated it. Bloomberg says December.
- **Siri AI in China.** No public timeline. The Baidu work is the thread to follow.
- **Visual Intelligence.** China Daily ties Baidu to it, but neither Cook nor Bloomberg's first-wave list mentions it for the initial release.

For Apple, the win today is narrow but real: after two years of "soon," its chairman stood in Shanghai and gave Chinese users a month. The harder part — making two-year-old features feel new in a market where rivals got there first — starts with that beta.

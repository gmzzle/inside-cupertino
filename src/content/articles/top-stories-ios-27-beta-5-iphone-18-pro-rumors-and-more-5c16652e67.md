We're five betas into iOS 27 and Apple's still adjusting app icons and tweaking Liquid Glass - the visual effect they introduced back in iOS 26 that nobody asked for but everyone has opinions about. [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/15/top-stories-ios-27-beta-5/) walked through the changes this week, and honestly, icon redesigns this late in the beta cycle is classic Apple. They'll obsess over a five-pixel shift in a corner radius while leaving actually broken things for a point release.

The timing here matters. We're about a month from the iPhone 18 event, which means iOS 27 is basically locked except for last-minute panic fixes. Manufacturing's already ramping up. These beta 5 tweaks are the software equivalent of rearranging deck chairs - surface polish while the engineering team is probably fighting fires we won't hear about until iOS 27.1.

Meanwhile, someone leaked a photo of what's allegedly the iPhone 18 Pro Max battery, claiming it's 12% larger than the 17 Pro Max. Take that with the usual grain of salt - battery component leaks are easy to fake and hard to verify. But if it's real, a 12% bump would put the 18 Pro Max somewhere around 5,200 mAh, which would be Apple's largest iPhone battery ever. That tracks with the rumors about slightly thicker chassis dimensions and the push for better battery life after the 17 Pro Max's... underwhelming stamina.

What's actually interesting is what Apple does with that extra capacity. They could just give us more screen-on time, which would be the sane move. Or they could burn it on a brighter display, more aggressive ProMotion, or whatever AI features they're cooking up that'll inevitably drain batteries faster than they admit. Apple's never met a battery improvement they couldn't immediately offset with new power-hungry features.

Beta 5 icon tweaks won't move the needle, but a meaningfully bigger battery might actually matter to people who use their phones.

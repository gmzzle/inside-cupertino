[AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/07/25/micron-urges-white-house-to-reject-apples-blacklist-memory-plan?utm_source=rss) reports that Micron CEO Sanjay Mehrotra personally lobbied Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick against Apple's June petition to buy Mac RAM from a blacklisted Chinese supplier. Micron's argument: letting Apple carve out exceptions for blacklisted firms will destabilize the U.S. tech industry.

Which is corporate speak for "we don't want to compete with cheaper Chinese RAM that Apple gets special permission to buy." Micron has a point about enforcement consistency - if Apple gets a pass, why wouldn't Samsung or Dell or HP line up next? But the underlying issue is that Micron simply cannot produce enough high-end memory at the volume Apple needs, especially for the kind of unified memory configurations the M-series Macs require. Apple wouldn't be asking for an exemption if domestic supply actually worked.

The blacklist exists for national security reasons, and those concerns are real. But Apple's request wasn't for bleeding-edge AI chips or missile guidance systems - it was for commodity DRAM that goes into consumer laptops. The distinction matters. If U.S. policy is that tech companies must exclusively use American suppliers, then American suppliers need to actually be able to supply. Right now, they can't, at least not at the scale Apple operates.

Micron bringing the CEO to Washington signals how seriously they're taking this. They're probably right that Apple getting a carveout sets a bad precedent. But the Trump administration already handed out blacklist exceptions like candy during the first term, so the precedent argument feels a bit late. The real fight here is whether Apple's supply chain leverage is strong enough to override both Micron's domestic lobbying and the broader hawkish posture on China. Based on how these things usually go, Apple probably gets some version of what it wants, just with enough strings attached that everyone can claim a win.

John Ternus’s first fall keynote did exactly what Tim Cook’s machine was built to do: sell the expensive thing with a straight face and make the missing pieces feel intentional.

The headline is the iPhone Duo — Apple’s first foldable — at $1,999. Open it and you get a 7.6-inch canvas Apple swears is the thinnest iPhone ever made, nano-texture and all. Close it and you’re holding a 5.4-inch outer display that claims ninety percent of an iPhone 18 Pro’s screen area without the Pro’s honesty about being a normal phone. Same aspect ratio on both sides, so content “scales proportionally.” Apple Pencil on a phone. Split-screen. A hinge with more than a hundred engineered parts in Grade 5 aluminum. Dual batteries. A vapor chamber. The A20 Pro. Rear-camera selfies with the cover screen as a viewfinder. Preorders October 16. In stores October 23.

None of that is the story.

The story is the number. Two thousand dollars is not a catch-up price. Samsung has been folding phones for years. Apple waited until the foldable could be a halo product — late enough to look deliberate, rich enough to keep the Pro lineup from looking silly by comparison. Ternus said it will “redefine the experience of using a foldable phone.” Redefining usually costs less when you’re second to market. Apple’s version of redefining is charging for the privilege of finally showing up.

Then there’s what didn’t get a slide. No base iPhone 18. The event skipped the volume phone and went straight to iPhone 18 Pro ($1,199) and Pro Max ($1,299) — another hundred dollars up on the Max — with the “biggest camera improvement in years”: a 48-megapixel variable aperture, proper Pro controls, texture and grain in Photographic Styles. Fine. Useful for people who already live in the Pro lane. Combined with a two-grand Duo and no entry model on stage, it reads less like a lineup and more like a filter. If you’re not spending at least twelve hundred, this event was not for you.

Siri AI got the privacy sermon Apple always gives when competitors are louder and further along. English beta now; more languages in October; claims of action across hundreds of thousands of apps. iOS 27 will let you dial back Liquid Glass opacity — a small admission that last year’s aesthetic needed a volume knob. The software story is competent continuity. It is not a reason to buy a hinge.

The quieter product tension is on the wrist. Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4 pick up Sound Recognition and “Audio Intelligence”: Live Rewind of the last fifteen seconds of a conversation as text, plus Siri Recap for meetings and parent-teacher nights. Helpful if you miss things. Unsettling if you think about where the audio buffer lives and what “remember that for me” trains people to accept. Apple will say on-device and private until the demos get cute. Treat the demos as demos.

AirPods 5 at $129 with stronger ANC and stem volume on the cheap model are the most honest announcement of the day: incremental, priced like a real upgrade, shipping September 18 with the Pros. The redesigned Health app — Readiness, Health Age, Longevity — arrives later, U.S. English first. Scoreboards for living longer. We’ll see if the metrics survive contact with actual doctors.

Ternus took over September 1. Eight days later he hosted “Surprise and Shine” like he’d been doing it for a decade. That is the point. This was not a break with Cook. It was proof the apparatus doesn’t need Cook on stage to ship a premium narrative: skip the cheap phone, raise the Pro floor, put the foldable on a pedestal, and call the price a feature.

Who it’s for: people who want the first Apple foldable as a status object, and Pro buyers who were upgrading anyway. Who it’s not for: anyone waiting for Apple to compete on foldable value, or anyone who noticed the base iPhone didn’t get a chair at the table.

The Duo will photograph beautifully. At $1,999, it had better.

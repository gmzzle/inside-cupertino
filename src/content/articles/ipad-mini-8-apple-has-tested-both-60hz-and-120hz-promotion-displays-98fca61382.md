[MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/15/ipad-mini-8-120hz-tested/) reports that Apple has tested both 60Hz and 120Hz displays for the iPad mini 8, per Mark Gurman. Which is the kind of scoop that sounds significant until you remember that Apple's industrial design teams test everything. They probably tested ceramic backs and titanium frames too. Testing isn't choosing.

The actual decision comes down to cost and positioning. ProMotion has been an iPad Pro exclusive since 2017, then migrated to the Air last year. Bringing it to the mini would complete the lineup, sure, but it would also eliminate one of the remaining reasons to spend $599 on an Air instead of $499 on a mini. Apple's not usually in the business of cannibalizing upmarket.

Then there's the OLED factor. Gurman mentions the mini 8 is rumored to get an OLED panel when it launches later this month, which would already be a substantial bill-of-materials jump from LCD. Stacking ProMotion on top of that pushes the mini into a pricing bind. Either Apple eats margin to keep it at $499, or they bump it to $549-$599 and suddenly it's iPad Air money for a smaller screen. The mini's appeal has always been size and value. Pricing it like a flagship undercuts the whole point.

The smart play is 60Hz OLED at $499, or maybe $529 if Apple feels bold. It's a meaningful upgrade without wrecking the product's identity. ProMotion would be nice, obviously, but the mini's buyers aren't the ones refreshing Geekbench scores. They want portability and Apple Pencil support without spending Air money. If Apple tested both refresh rates, the 60Hz version probably wins on those terms alone.

Google Wallet is back on the iPhone. [Google announced](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/platforms/google-pay/wallet-available-ios-united-states/) on Tuesday that its Wallet app is now available to iOS users across the US. As [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1035592/google-wallet-ios-iphone-mobile-app-us-availability) points out, it's been a little over 13 years since the app first showed up on iPhone.

There's one big catch, and Google put it right in the announcement: "tap-to-pay functionality isn't supported on iOS at this time."

So if you were hoping to hold your iPhone up to a card reader and pay with Google instead of Apple Pay, you can't. Here's what the app *does* do, and why Google bothered.

## What Google Wallet on iPhone can do

Per Google's post and [9to5Google's hands-on](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/17/google-wallet-ios-app/):

- **Pull in your stuff from Gmail.** Boarding passes, concert tickets, package tracking, and receipts sync automatically from Gmail and other Google apps.
- **Scan physical cards.** Point the camera at a loyalty card or gym membership to add it. There's also an "Everything else" scanner for cards that don't fit a category.
- **Store payment cards for online checkout.** Cards saved to your Google account work for faster checkout across Google and anywhere Google Pay is accepted online, including in Chrome.
- **Track purchases.** A transactions tab lists recent Google-linked purchases, and the app summarizes purchase emails.

According to [APPDOOK](https://appdook.com/news/apple-news-roundup-2026-11-18), the app is free, about 171 MB, and requires iOS 17 or later.

## The real reason it exists: AI shopping

Google is direct about the bigger goal. It wants Wallet to be "a secure hub for managing agentic purchases across Google," meaning order confirmations, receipts, and delivery updates for purchases its AI tools make on your behalf.

That's the part Apple Wallet doesn't really try to do. As [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2319673/google-wallet-is-launching-on-ios/) notes, most of the rest overlaps heavily with the Wallet app already on your phone. Bringing Wallet to iPhone means Google's AI shopping features can follow its users no matter which phone they carry.

## Why no tap-to-pay?

This part is less clear than it sounds. Apple has let US developers build contactless payments into their own apps since iOS 18.1 through its NFC and SE Platform. But it comes with conditions: a commercial agreement with Apple, which [APPDOOK](https://appdook.com/news/apple-news-roundup-2026-11-18) notes Apple doesn't publish the terms or fees for, plus payment licensing and security review requirements.

Google hasn't said which of those, if any, is holding things up, or whether tap-to-pay is coming at all. "At this time" leaves the door open, but there's no date.

## Should you install it?

**Yes, if you live in Gmail.** If your flights, tickets, and orders already land in a Google account, having them auto-collected in one app is genuinely handy, especially if you share a household with Android users.

**Maybe, if you use Google Pay online a lot.** Having your saved cards and recent Google purchases in one place is nice, but it's not a big change from using Chrome.

**No, if you want to replace Apple Pay.** Google Wallet can't help you at the register, so Apple Pay is still how you'll tap your iPhone to pay.

Either way, it's a small sign of how the next payments fight is shaping up. It's less about who owns the NFC chip and more about whose AI is placing the order.

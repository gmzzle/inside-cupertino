If you bought an iPhone 16 or an iPhone 15 Pro in the US expecting the smarter Siri Apple showed off in 2024, there is now a better idea of what Apple's class action settlement might pay you. And there's one month left to claim it.

According to [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/21/apple-siri-ai-settlement-payment-amount/), lawyers for the class said in a November 16 court document that claimants will likely receive at least $61 per eligible iPhone, based on early participation data. That's well above the $25 per device the settlement starts from. The claim deadline is **December 21, 2026**.

The number is real, but it isn't a promise. Here's what's actually going on.

## Where this came from

In June 2024, Apple previewed a more personal Siri powered by Apple Intelligence: one that understood your personal context and what was on your screen, and could act inside apps. Apple advertised those features in presentations, on its website, and in a TV ad. In March 2025 it delayed them, and a false advertising class action followed.

In May, Apple agreed to pay $250 million to settle. It denies all of the allegations. In a statement MacRumors quotes, Apple said it settled "to stay focused" on "delivering the most innovative products and services to our users."

The irony is that the feature at the center of the case now exists. Apple announced Siri AI at WWDC in June, and it runs on the iPhone 15 Pro and newer with iOS 27. But MacRumors notes it is still labeled a beta, and as we [covered earlier this week](/articles/siri-ai-daily-limits-icloud-plus-tiers-5b8e1c37/), iOS 27.3 gives it a daily usage cap. So the people being paid for a delayed feature are getting it late and with limits.

## Who qualifies

The [official settlement website](https://www.smartphoneaisettlement.com/) spells out the rules. You need to:

- **Live in the US and have bought the iPhone in the US** between June 10, 2024, and March 29, 2025.
- **Be the original purchaser**, and not have bought it to resell.
- **Own one of these models:** iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 15 Pro Max, iPhone 16, iPhone 16e, iPhone 16 Plus, iPhone 16 Pro, or iPhone 16 Pro Max.

The regular iPhone 15 and iPhone 15 Plus aren't on the list. Buyers outside the US, including in Canada, aren't covered either.

## How to file

Claims go through [smartphoneaisettlement.com](https://www.smartphoneaisettlement.com/). MacRumors says eligible customers should already have received an email with a claim ID and PIN, but you can file without them. The form asks for your personal details and the iPhone's serial number. If you don't have the serial number, it asks for your Apple Account email and the phone's number instead.

Two details are easy to miss:

1. **One form per phone.** The settlement site says that if you bought more than one eligible device, you must submit a separate claim form for each.
2. **You are confirming something.** According to the site, the claim form asks you to confirm that you bought the phone expecting certain Siri Apple Intelligence features that you didn't receive. If that wasn't you, this isn't free money you're entitled to.

To be safe, go to the settlement site directly rather than through a link in a text or a random email.

## Why $61 is a forecast, not a floor

This is the part the headlines skip. The settlement site says the $25 per device "may be increased pro rata, up to a maximum of $95 per Eligible Device, or decreased pro rata depending on the total number of valid claims submitted and other factors."

In plain terms, a fixed pot gets split among everyone who files. Fewer claims means a bigger check for each person, up to $95. More claims means a smaller one. According to the site, the costs of notice and settlement administration, taxes, litigation costs, and any attorneys' fees and service awards also come out of the $250 million before class members are paid.

Because the per-phone amount rises when fewer people file, an estimate of $61 tells you participation so far has been modest. MacRumors says the lawyers expect at least $61 unless there's a significant uptick in claims. With a month to go and the estimate now making headlines, an uptick is possible. So $61 is a reasonable guess for today, not a guarantee for February.

## What happens next

- **December 21, 2026:** Last day to file a claim, opt out, or object.
- **February 24, 2027:** The court holds its final approval hearing.
- **After that:** Payments, at some point in 2027 if the judge approves the deal.

If you qualify, filing takes a few minutes and costs nothing. Just keep your expectations reasonable. This is a refund on a promise Apple has only partly kept, and the amount depends on how many people take the money.

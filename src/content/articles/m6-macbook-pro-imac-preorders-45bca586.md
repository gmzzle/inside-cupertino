Day two of Apple's "exciting week," and it's the Mac's turn. Early Tuesday, a day after the [OLED iPad mini](/articles/new-ipad-mini-oled-a20-pro-ip67-7f579175/), Apple announced by press release a [14-inch MacBook Pro with M6](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/apple-introduces-the-new-14-inch-macbook-pro-with-m6/) and a [new iMac with M6](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/apple-unveils-new-imac-with-m6/). Both are spec bumps rather than redesigns. The headline is what *didn't* happen: neither starting price went up.

These aren't the touchscreen machines. Those are still expected at Apple's ["Pro and tell" event](/articles/apple-pro-and-tell-october-28-touchscreen-macbook-pro-ef11fa66/) in Los Angeles on Wednesday. Today's two are the base 14-inch MacBook Pro and the colorful 24-inch iMac.

## 14-inch MacBook Pro with M6

- **More cores.** M6 here has a 12-core CPU and a 12-core GPU, two more of each than the M5 model, per Apple.
- **Apple's speed claims.** Up to 30 percent faster graphics and up to 25 percent faster on-device AI work (like LLM prompt processing) than M5. Apple says the Neural Engine, now a "Dual 16-core" design, is up to 1.9x faster than M5's. In app tests it cites roughly 1.2x to 1.3x gains over M5 in Blender, Affinity and Xcode.
- **Battery.** Up to 24 hours, which Apple calls the longest ever in a Mac.
- **Memory and storage.** 16GB of unified memory standard, configurable to 32GB, with up to 170GB/s of bandwidth. [MacRumors reports](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/27/m6-14-inch-macbook-pro-pre-orders-release-date/) the base model ships with 1TB of storage, configurable to 4TB.
- **Wireless.** Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6.
- **What carries over.** The Liquid Retina XDR display (up to 1,600 nits HDR, with a nano-texture option), the 12MP Center Stage camera, six speakers, HDMI, MagSafe 3, three Thunderbolt 4 ports, an SDXC slot and a headphone jack. Space black or silver.

**Price:** from $1,999, or $1,899 with education pricing.

## iMac with M6

- **Skips M5 entirely.** The last iMac had M4, and this is the first iMac update in two years, as [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1018467/apple-imac-m6-chip-announced-price-release) and [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/27/m6-imac-pre-orders-release-date/) both point out. So the jump is bigger here than on the laptop.
- **Apple's speed claims.** Up to 12-core CPU and 12-core GPU, both two more cores than before. Apple says up to 2x faster graphics and up to 5x faster on-device AI performance versus the M4 iMac, and up to 18x faster than the most popular Intel-based iMac.
- **Connectivity.** Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6, and The Verge notes these are now on the base model too. Gigabit Ethernet becomes 2.5 Gigabit on 12-core models, and Apple says the SSD is up to 2x faster.
- **Same look.** Same 24-inch 4.5K Retina display and the same seven colors: blue, green, pink, yellow, purple, orange and silver.

**Price:** $1,499 for the 10-core CPU/10-core GPU model with two Thunderbolt ports, or $1,699 for the 12-core version with four. Education pricing is $1,449 and $1,599. MacRumors says both start with 16GB of memory and 256GB of storage.

**Check the build page carefully.** The Verge spotted some new charges. Swapping the Magic Mouse for a Magic Trackpad is now a $50 upgrade where it used to be free, and the faster Ethernet is a $30 option. It also says you can't get a Touch ID keyboard without a numeric keypad.

## Dates

Pre-orders for both open **Wednesday, October 28, at 10:10 a.m. Pacific**, in 35 countries and regions including the U.S. Deliveries and in-store availability start **Friday, November 6**. That's the [store-reset date Gurman flagged](/articles/apple-four-launch-fridays-november-6-macs-5c81d2a4/) earlier this month.

## "No price hike" needs context

TechRadar's headline calls it [good news that there are no price hikes](https://www.techradar.com/computing/macbooks/apple-reveals-imac-m6-and-macbook-pro-m6-and-the-good-news-is-there-are-no-price-hikes), and that's true compared with what Apple charged yesterday. But the 14-inch MacBook Pro already cost $1,999 before today, and [MacRumors notes](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/27/m6-14-inch-macbook-pro-pre-orders-release-date/) that the 1TB base storage only looks generous because Apple dropped the 512GB option in March. So $1,999 isn't a bargain. It just isn't a fresh increase. Getting two more CPU and GPU cores, and Wi-Fi 7 for the same money is real progress, but the base 14-inch Pro is still a $2,000 laptop.

## Should you buy one?

**iMac owners on M1 or Intel:** This is the one to buy. Skipping M5 means a real jump, and the design, colors and display you already like haven't changed.

**M4 iMac owners:** Unless you run local AI models or edit a lot of photos and video, Apple's own numbers don't make a strong case for upgrading after two years.

**Anyone buying a 14-inch MacBook Pro:** Wait one day. Apple is expected to show its touchscreen MacBook Pro on Wednesday, and pre-orders don't open until 10:10 a.m. that day anyway. Earlier reporting [summarized by 9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/18/new-macbook-pro-with-touchscreen-and-oled-november-6-release/) says that machine sticks with M5 Pro and M5 Max chips. So the real decision may be a cheaper M6 laptop with a classic screen versus a pricier M5 Pro/Max laptop with an OLED touchscreen. You'll know the trade-off by lunchtime tomorrow.

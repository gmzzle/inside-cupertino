The HomePod mini launched in November 2020. The current Apple TV 4K shipped in October 2022, but its predecessor hung around for five years before that. If you've been waiting for Apple to care about the smart home again, [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/15/apple-home-product-roadmap-tv-homepod-smart-display/) says that wait is almost over.

The holdup, apparently, was Siri. Apple didn't want to ship new home hardware until the AI-powered version of Siri was ready, which makes sense if you squint. Nobody wants to launch a $300 speaker that sounds dated the moment iOS 18.4 drops. But the multi-year delay meant Apple ceded ground to Google and Amazon at exactly the moment smart displays and Matter hubs became table stakes. The Apple TV is still the best TV box you can buy, but it's also still using the A15 from the iPhone 13.

Now that Siri AI is shipping—or at least shipping enough for Apple's standards—the pipeline is supposedly filling up. We're looking at refreshed HomePods, a new Apple TV, and the long-rumored smart display that's been "just around the corner" since 2023. The smart display is the most interesting piece. Amazon has half a dozen Echo Shows at different price points. Google has the Nest Hub Max. Apple has... an iPad you can prop up with a case. If they're serious about the home, a first-party display with a dock, better speakers, and HomeKit/Matter controls baked in would actually be competitive.

The risk is that Apple launches all of this into a smart home market that's already figured out what it wants, and it's not premium hardware. The HomePod mini was well-received because it was $99. The original HomePod at $349 was a flop. If the new hardware lineup is priced like Apple still thinks it's 2018, it won't matter how good Siri sounds when you ask it to dim the lights.

The smart home has been Apple's neglected stepchild for half a decade, and that's left HomeKit feeling like a hobby project. New hardware won't fix that alone, but it's a start—assuming they actually ship it this time.

Black Friday is November 27, and Apple's holiday shopping season is already underway. If you're planning to buy a gift at an Apple Store in the next few weeks, there's one thing worth knowing before you walk up to the counter: some customers may have walked out with paid subscription trials they never clearly agreed to.

Here's what's been alleged, what Apple has changed, and the two-minute check that keeps a surprise charge off your card.

## What happened

Over the past few weeks, repair technician and consumer-rights advocate Louis Rossmann published a series of YouTube videos built on tips from Apple retail employees. As [MacRumors laid out](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/08/apple-store-subscription-controversy/), the process at U.S. Apple Stores works like this:

1. A staff member asks if you'd like to scan a QR code for a personalized shopping experience, which Apple calls **Connect and Personalize**.
2. If you agree, the employee's company device can show which Apple devices you own, which Apple services you already pay for, and which subscription trials you qualify for based on your purchase.
3. The trial offers appear with **Agree** and **Decline** buttons on the *employee's* device, not your phone. The default payment method on your Apple Account is already attached.

According to the tipsters, some employees rushed through that screen, described trials as "gifts," or in some cases tapped Agree themselves without getting real consent. The trials then roll into paid, auto-renewing subscriptions unless you cancel. [9to5Mac notes](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/09/apple-staff-accused-of-signing-up-customers-to-subscriptions-without-consent-company-responds/) that buying an iPhone currently comes with a three-month Apple TV trial, so a single purchase can come with real recurring dollars attached.

Why would staff do this? Until early this month, Apple tracked a **"services attach rate"** for retail employees, with a target of 40% at at least some U.S. stores, meaning four of every ten customers helped with a device purchase were supposed to sign up for at least one subscription.

## What Apple changed

MacRumors confirmed Apple removed the services metric for retail staff in early November, just days after Rossmann's videos went up. Apple's official explanation is that the metric is less relevant during the holidays, when people are often buying for someone else.

Apple also sent retail employees an internal memo about respecting customer privacy and getting consent, covering subscription trials and Connect and Personalize, plus Apple Upgrade sign-ups and Genius Bar repair authorizations. Per Rossmann's follow-up, as reported by 9to5Mac, staff were told only the customer should press Accept.

Whatever the reason for the timing, it landed right before Apple brings on seasonal hires for its busiest weeks of the year.

## How to protect yourself in the store

- **You can say no to the QR code.** Connect and Personalize is optional. Skip it and the employee has less to work with.
- **Read the screen before anything is tapped.** If an employee turns a device toward you with trial offers, read what each one is and when billing would start. Decline anything you don't plan to use.
- **Ask directly:** "Is anything being added to my account today?" It's a fair question, and the answer should be easy.
- **Watch your inbox.** Apple emails you when a new trial starts on your account. That email is easy to dismiss as a receipt, so open it.

## The two-minute check to do today

Even if you haven't shopped at an Apple Store lately, it's worth a look, especially if you bought a device there this year.

1. On your iPhone, open **Settings** and tap **your name** at the top.
2. Tap **Subscriptions**. You'll see every active subscription and trial on your Apple Account.
3. Tap anything you don't want, then tap **Cancel Subscription** (or **Cancel All Services** for an Apple One bundle).

If you've already been charged for something you never agreed to, go to [reportaproblem.apple.com](https://reportaproblem.apple.com), choose **Request a refund**, and follow the steps.

## The bigger picture

Services are now a core part of Apple's business, and every trial that quietly converts adds to that revenue. Dropping the attach-rate target is a good step, but it puts the burden back where it always was: on you to check what's on your account. Set a reminder for the day before any trial you *do* want ends, and look over your card statement after the holidays.

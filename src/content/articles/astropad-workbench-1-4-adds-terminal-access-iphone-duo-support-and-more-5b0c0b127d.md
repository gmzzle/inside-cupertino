[9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/27/astropad-workbench-1-4-adds-terminal-access-iphone-duo-support-and-more/) reported that Workbench 1.4 now includes "a built-in terminal" and native iPhone support. Those two additions transform what was already a solid remote desktop tool into something genuinely useful for people who actually need to get work done away from their desk.

The terminal is the key piece here. Most remote desktop apps treat you like you're controlling a mouse from afar, which is fine for dragging files around but terrible for anyone who spends time in command-line workflows. Workbench already had an edge over Apple's own Screen Sharing by running natively on iPad instead of through a browser-style interface. Now you can SSH into your Mac without juggling a separate terminal app or trying to tap your way through vim on a touch keyboard. That's the difference between a tool you tolerate and one you actually choose to use.

The iPhone Duo support is more niche but signals where Astropad thinks this is going. Duo mode lets you use an iPhone as a second input device alongside your iPad, sort of like a tiny wireless numpad or macro pad. It launched for iPad earlier this year and honestly felt like a feature looking for a problem. But if you're already controlling a Mac from an iPad and need quick access to specific shortcuts or scripts, having your iPhone sitting there as a custom control surface starts to make sense. It's the kind of workflow only a few hundred people will use, but those people will *really* use it.

Astropad's also added a new file transfer system between Macs, which suggests they're thinking about Workbench less as "remote desktop for creatives" and more as a full remote management tool. The company made its name with the original Astropad app that turned iPads into drawing tablets for Macs, so this pivot toward IT-adjacent features is notable. They're chasing the audience that wants Screen Sharing's convenience with more actual functionality, and with Apple showing zero interest in meaningfully updating its own tools, there's room.

Workbench still requires a subscription, which remains annoying for software that's essentially SSH with a nice UI. But at least now it's SSH with a nice UI and an actual terminal.

Plenty of people already type their symptoms into a chatbot. Apple wants to find out whether that can be done more safely, and it's asking iPhone owners to help.

On Sunday, Apple [announced the Apple Health AI Usage Study](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/11/apple-launches-research-study-to-explore-the-use-of-ai-for-health/), a U.S. research study built around a new iPhone app that answers everyday health questions. It's run with OpenEvidence, a clinical AI company whose tool is already widely used by doctors. Enrollment is open now, and Apple is looking for up to 200,000 participants.

Here's what the app does, who can join, and what to think about before you tap Enroll.

## What the app actually does

You type a health question and get an answer written in plain language, with links to the peer-reviewed sources behind it. Apple says those answers draw on medical literature from journals such as *The New England Journal of Medicine*, *JAMA*, and *Nature*, rather than whatever is most popular on the web.

Apple pitches a few everyday uses:

- **Making sense of a symptom or a diagnosis** you've already been given.
- **Putting your Health app data in context**, such as resting heart rate, sleep, or lab results.
- **Getting ready for a doctor's appointment** so you walk in with better questions.
- **Building exercise or meal plans** tailored to you.

You'll rate the answers and fill out occasional surveys. [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/15/apple-launches-new-app-to-study-how-people-use-ai-for-health/) the surveys come monthly and that Apple plans to run the study for more than 12 months.

One thing it isn't: Siri. [The Verge notes](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1033355/apples-new-study-looks-to-improve-the-effectiveness-of-ai-health-tools) the app is text-based and isn't tied into Siri or Apple Intelligence, though you can dictate with the keyboard.

## Who can join

According to Apple, you need to be:

- **21 or older** and living in the **United States**
- Using an **iPhone 15 Pro or later** (an Apple Intelligence–capable model)
- Running **iOS 26 or later**

If that's you, search the App Store for the **Apple Health AI Study** app. It walks you through the study details before you decide to enroll. Readers outside the U.S., including in Canada, can't join for now.

## What data it sees, and what happens to it

This is the part worth reading slowly, because it's your health information.

- **Sharing Health data is optional.** During setup you choose whether to share HealthKit data such as activity, sleep, resting heart rate, or lab results. You can join without sharing any of it. The trade-off is that answers will be less personal.
- **Questions are stripped of identifying details** before they reach OpenEvidence, according to Apple, and OpenEvidence deletes question-and-response data within 24 hours.
- **Study data won't be used to train AI models**, Apple says.
- **Survey answers are your call.** You control whether you share them.

The app also has guardrails meant to catch worrying questions and point you to a healthcare provider or other resources. Apple is clear that it doesn't diagnose or treat anything, and it isn't a replacement for your doctor.

## Should you sign up?

It comes down to how you already use AI for health questions.

**It's worth a look if** you're already asking general chatbots about symptoms or test results. This app shows its sources, so you can check the claim yourself, and Apple has put its privacy rules in writing.

**Think twice if** you're uneasy about any company seeing health questions, even stripped of identifying details. You can still join without sharing Health data, but every question you ask still goes to OpenEvidence for processing.

Either way, treat the answers as homework for your next appointment, not as the appointment.

## The bigger picture

A study of up to 200,000 people is a careful way for Apple to learn what makes people trust, or misread, an AI answer. Apple announced the study at the HLTH conference in Las Vegas, [Healthcare Dive reports](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/apple-openevidence-conversational-health-ai-study-app/874714/), and says the findings are meant to shape health AI design both for Apple and for the industry. If you join, your ratings are part of what shapes those future tools.
